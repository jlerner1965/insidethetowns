# Photo permissions

Permission granted to publish someone else's photographs, recorded verbatim.

`IMAGE_LICENSES.csv` carries one row per image file. Where an image is covered
by a grant below, its `license` column cites the section here rather than
restating the terms, so the wording lives in exactly one place.

A grant is only worth what its wording says. Record the sentence, who sent it,
their role and the date — not a paraphrase, and not a licence name the sender
did not actually use.

---

## Town of Erie — parks and open space

**Granted** 18 September 2026 by **Ashley Burger, Communications & Marketing
Manager**, Town of Erie (Communications & Community Engagement), by email, in
reply to a photo permission request sent the same morning.

Her wording, in full:

> Yes, you can absolutely use an of the photos of our parks and open space that
> are on our website, without photo credit. They were taken by Town staff and
> are free for public use. If you are looking for particular photos of our
> parks that aren't on the website, please reach out, I likely already have
> them.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Photographs of Erie parks and open space published on erieco.gov |
| **Not covered** | Events, downtown, businesses, or anything the Town did not shoot |
| **Credit** | Not required. We credit "Town of Erie" anyway |
| **Standing** | Yes — not limited to one request |
| **Contact** | 303-926-2541 |

"Free for public use" is her characterisation, not a named licence. Colorado
municipal works are not automatically public domain the way federal works are,
so what permits publication here is this grant from an officer authorised to
give it — which is why the sentence is recorded rather than summarised.

Five Erie parks still have no photograph because none is published on
erieco.gov: Arapahoe Ridge, Lehigh, Longs Peak, Serene and Star Meadows, plus
the Erie Singletrack at Sunset Open Space, whose page carries only trail maps.
Her offer to dig out unpublished photographs covers these.
