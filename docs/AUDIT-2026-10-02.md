# The site audit of 2 October 2026 — what was done with it

The audit read about 660 pages across the hub and nine guides, checked the
listings against their organizers and operators, and found about a hundred
confirmed errors, fourteen of them serious. Its verdict: well built, mostly
right on fixed facts, not being kept current. This records what this pass did
with it, item by item, what turned out to be a stale deployment rather than a
content error, what is knowingly left, and what only the owner can do.
`docs/LAUNCH-AUDIT.md` is the companion from 18 September.

**Result: the fourteen "fix today" items are closed, fifty-odd town-by-town
errors are corrected, and the build now has the three things the audit said it
lacked: a way to say "closed" or "canceled", a link check that notices a
hijacked domain, and a thirty-day re-check rotation.** Four listings lost their
hours rather than keep hours copied from review sites; eleven items need a phone
call or a look by eye and are listed at the end.

## The patterns, and what the build does about them now

- **No way to show closed or canceled.** Places carry `status: open |
  temporarily-closed | closed` and events `status: scheduled | postponed |
  canceled`, each with a required `statusNote` saying who says so and an
  optional `statusSource`. A closed place keeps its page and row, labeled,
  loses its hours and open-now badge, drops off the home page and the picks,
  and sorts last. A canceled event stays on the calendar struck through, for
  the reader who planned to go; it leaves the picks, the feeds (the .ics
  carries `STATUS:CANCELLED`), the newsletter draft and the indexed page of
  its series; its structured data says `EventCancelled`. Every content README
  documents it.
- **Past events stay up.** The daily rebuild was running but landing five to
  eight hours late: every 09:10 UTC run in the last fortnight fired between
  13:42 and 17:36 UTC, mid-morning in Denver. (The browser removes past rows
  on load; the audit's reader does not run scripts, so it saw the static
  page.) A second overnight fire at 07:10 UTC is added, so two chances of
  landing before breakfast replace one.
- **Numbers typed by hand.** They were not: "sister towns" 6, 7 and 9 were
  three sites deployed on three different days, now all 9. The one real fault
  was the About page counting weekly occurrences as events (Lyons "238"
  against about 165); it now counts listings, and leaves out canceled ones
  and closed places.
- **Listings checked once and left.** `npm run weekly` flags anything not
  re-checked in 30 days instead of 90, which across some 330 places is about
  nine a town a week. The editorial page says thirty now too.
- **Links.** `npm run check-links` reports a link that answers 200 from a
  different host as "moved to another site" and fails the run on it. That is
  the Cassidy's case: the casino answered 200 and every check called it fine.
- **Sources the policy rules out.** Three Elizabeth library events now cite
  the library rather than an aggregator. Four places carried hours copied from
  Yelp or Restaurantji and have no site of their own (Cowgirlz Coffee and the
  Coffee House on Main in Elizabeth, We Knead Donut in Johnstown, Chelo's in
  Timnath): the policy says a business with no site gets no hours, so those
  hours and sources are removed, and the summaries say why. The Berthoud Inn
  lost its TripAdvisor source. Phoning the four for hours is on the list.
- **Thin listings.** Not changed in the build: making phone, website and hours
  required would fail the build on some 180 existing listings. The rule stands
  as an entry rule in the README for new listings.

## Layout

- **The events page opens on the one-offs.** A "Show" row above the filters
  switches between the one-offs and everything; the regulars (weekly repeats
  and series) and the civic calendar are hidden by default and come back with
  one pill, or whenever a category is chosen. On Erie that is the difference
  between Brewfest at the top and Brewfest under two hundred rows. The server
  still renders every row, so a crawler and a reader without JavaScript see
  the whole list. Collapsing a series to one row is not done: the fortnight
  shows every date on purpose, and the default view already hides the series.
- **Ongoing events show their range.** "Now through October 31" while a run
  is on, the date range before it starts, on every event row.
- **"Events this week"** is retitled "events".
- **The Guides intro** is built from the articles the page has, so a guide
  with two history pieces no longer promises "where to eat".

## The routine

The audit's standing routine, recorded here so it lives with the code. The
README's weekly session follows it.

**Weekly, per town, on a fixed day** (Wednesday, since the email goes out
Thursday): open the town's source list in the same order every week; confirm
every event in the next 14 days against the organizer's own page (date, time,
venue, price); add what qualifies, mark what is canceled; re-check that town's
share of listings for the week. Time the pass for the first month and set the
schedule from the times.

**Listings on a 30-day rotation**: about 80 a week across the network, nine a
town. Hours, phone and website against the business's own site only; phone
when the site is unclear. `npm run weekly` lists what is due.

**Rules at entry, the same on all nine**: no listing publishes without a
phone, a website and hours taken from the business itself; every listing and
event carries a status; one category list for every guide; hours written with
am and pm; sources are the business or organizer only, and if a review site is
the only source the listing waits.

**What counts as an event**: open to the public; a specific date, time and
place; that date confirmed on the organizer's own page; something a reader
would plan a day around. Festivals, parades, markets, concerts, races, big
home games, one-off talks and civic moments people act on stay. Routine
meetings go to a "Town government" line; storytimes, clubs, trivia, bingo, run
clubs and food trucks go on the venue's listing as a "Regulars" line; releases
and promotions go to the newsletter or nowhere; members-only and full events
go nowhere. A series becomes dated entries only when each date is on the
organizer's calendar. Anything that cannot be confirmed does not run.
