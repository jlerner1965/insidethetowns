The Historical Society of Idaho Springs runs the Heritage Museum and Visitor Center at 2060 Miner Street, a 9,800-square-foot building the society says was planned in 1991 on the site of an abandoned swimming pool and first used in September 1992, as classrooms while Carlson Elementary was renovated. The visitor center gives out information on the town and sells crafts and souvenirs; the City of Idaho Springs' visitor site says its gift shop carries more than 35 Colorado artists, and that the museum has exhibits on mining history and early life, an extensive mineral display, a giant teepee, local wildlife and 10th Mountain Division training equipment from Camp Hale. There is a nominal fee for the museum. The society also describes the Mount Blue Sky road, "the highest paved road in North America", as starting at the visitor center.

The society says the center is open seven days a week, with winter hours of 10 am to 4 pm and summer hours of 9:30 am to 5 pm, but gives no dates for the change of season; the open-now status here uses 10 am to 4 pm, the hours common to both. Call 303-567-4382 before arriving near either end of the day.
