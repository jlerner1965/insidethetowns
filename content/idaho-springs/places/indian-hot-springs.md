Indian Hot Springs is the reason Idaho Springs has the name. The resort's main pool is geothermal mineral water, 96 to 102°F by the resort's own figure (it cannot adjust the temperature), under a sparkling dome planted with banana trees, ferns and flowers kept by a staff botanist; there are built-in benches around the edge and a snack area inside. Around it are private baths, outdoor tubs, Club Mud and the geothermal vapor caves (swimwear optional on weekdays, required at weekends), plus a hotel and a spa open 10 am to 7 pm.

The pool is open 8:30 am to 9 pm Monday to Friday with no reservation needed; at weekends it runs in sessions, 7:30 to 10 am, 10:30 am to 2 pm and 3 to 6:30 pm, with an adult swim from 7:30 to 9:30 pm, and weekend reservations are required. Pool admission on 14 October 2026 was $30 a person Monday to Thursday, $35 on Friday and $35 a time slot at weekends, with $10 off for soaking before 10 am Monday to Thursday and no discounts at weekends or holidays; children 2 and under are free with a paying adult, towels rent for $5 and key locks for $4. The private baths open 8 am to 9:30 pm and the outdoor tubs 8 am to 9 pm every day. There are no lifeguards, so anyone under 16 without a driver's license or permit must be with an adult; glass and coolers are not allowed. The resort's "Summit Pricing" dates also need a reservation.

The City of Idaho Springs' visitor site says the springs have welcomed visitors for over 150 years, that a Colorado historical grant funded a restoration plan begun in 2025, and that improvements planned for 2026 and beyond include new water systems, outdoor pools and kid-friendly areas, upgraded electrical, water and mechanical systems and better accommodations. The City's Virginia Canyon parking page points visitors to free Soda Creek parking north of the resort. The Historical Society of Idaho Springs traces the springs to Dr. Cummings' bath house of 1863 and the Ocean Bath House of 1871.
