Idaho Springs' history starts with the 1859 gold discovery associated with George Jackson and the Clear Creek valley. The town's own historical organization runs the local heritage museum, the place to start before the mining structures and downtown buildings.

## Let the museum set the questions

Begin at the [Heritage Museum and Visitor Center](/places/historical-society-of-idaho-springs-visitor-center/), for how the settlement grew from prospecting into a working town and how people lived alongside the creek and the mines.

Miner Street is the commercial center, and the guide's [walking route](/articles/explore-idaho-springs-in-walking-order/) links it with places to eat and pause. Many of its historic-looking buildings are still ordinary businesses or private spaces; interiors are open only where the operator offers a visit.

## Give the Argo its own time

The [Argo Mill and Tunnel](/places/argo-mill-and-tunnel/) tells the industrial side of the story. The operator describes its tours and books them through a calendar, and a timed tour is the fixed point to plan a day around.

The newer [Mighty Argo Cable Car](/places/mighty-argo-cable-car/) is a different attraction, with its own tickets and its own listing; check which one a ticket is for.

## Return to the creek

[Water Wheel Park](/places/water-wheel-park/) is a short stop by Clear Creek, and [Courtney-Ryley-Cooper Park](/places/courtney-ryley-cooper-park/) is another public park. Their listings cover access; not every path along the water is public.

With children, choose one interpretive visit and leave room for a park or library stop; the [family guide](/articles/idaho-springs-with-kids/) has the plan. In colder months, an indoor museum or tour is a separate decision from Mount Blue Sky or a high-country hike: a snow-free downtown says nothing about mountain-road access.
