Idaho Springs is on Clear Creek about 33 miles west of Denver on I-70, by the Mighty Argo's count, and the same operator puts the town at about a mile long and two streets wide, "because that's all the room the canyon gives it". Gold started it: in January 1859 George Jackson found placer gold where Chicago Creek meets Clear Creek, the first significant strike of the Colorado Gold Rush. Miner Street is, in the Argo's words, brick storefronts from the boom years, now full of breweries, restaurants, outfitters and coffee, and the Historical Society of Idaho Springs says the downtown was designated a National Historic District in 1983, with plaques on many buildings telling their history. This walk starts at the society's museum, takes the Argo's mill tour, and comes back down Miner Street's numbers to a meal. Park once, downtown or in the Argo's lots, and walk; the Argo itself sends drivers whose lot is full to public lots and street parking "within a few blocks".

## 01 · Heritage Museum and Visitor Center

The [Idaho Springs Heritage Museum and Visitor Center](/places/historical-society-of-idaho-springs-visitor-center/) at 2060 Miner Street is the Historical Society's, open seven days a week: 10 am–4 pm in winter and 9:30 am–5 pm in summer, though the society gives no date for the change, so call 303-567-4382 if you are arriving near either end of the day. There is a nominal fee for the museum. The City's visitor site lists exhibits on mining history and early life, an extensive mineral display and 10th Mountain Division training equipment from Camp Hale, and a gift shop with work by more than 35 Colorado artists. The society describes the [Mount Blue Sky road](/places/mount-blue-sky-scenic-byway/) as starting here; its upper part is closed for the season.

*Getting there:* 2060 Miner Street, the start of the walk. *Best for:* the background before the mill, and a map and answers for the rest of the day.

## 02 · Argo Mill & Tunnel

The [Argo Mill & Tunnel](/places/argo-mill-and-tunnel/) at 2350 Riverside Drive is the mill built in 1913 at the mouth of the 4.16-mile tunnel that drained the Central City mines, now run by the Mighty Argo. Guided tours follow the ore through the mill, much of its original equipment in place, and include gold or gemstone panning; they leave at the top of each hour from 10 am, the last at 3 pm, and the FAQ puts the whole visit at an hour and 20 minutes to an hour and a half. On 10 October 2026 the ticket table showed $32.95 for adults and $19.95 for ages 6 to 17. Walk-ins are taken, but tours fill, so book ahead. Dogs and strollers are not allowed on the tour, and sturdy closed-toe shoes are advised.

The [Mighty Argo Cable Car](/places/mighty-argo-cable-car/) leaves from the same base for Miners Point, about ten minutes each way, Sunday to Thursday 10 am–6 pm and Friday and Saturday 10 am–7 pm, the last passenger cabin up an hour before closing. It makes a second half to the day; the walk below assumes the mill only.

*Getting there:* on Riverside Drive, which the Argo says runs along Clear Creek through the middle of town; the mill is the large timber building on the north side, with the Argo's paid East and West lots beside the terminal. *Best for:* the mining story with the machinery in front of you; arrive by 3 pm.

## 03 · Water Wheel Park

[Water Wheel Park](/places/water-wheel-park/) is reached by a paved path from behind City Hall at 1711 Miner Street, which the City says leads under I-70 and along the creek to a better view of the wheel. The Historical Society calls it the Charlie Tayler Waterwheel: Tayler built it in 1893 to power a stamp mill at his mine on Ute Creek, it was moved here in 1946 as a gift from his estate, and volunteers restored it in 1988. Behind City Hall too, the City's Anderson Park holds Colorado & Southern locomotive No. 60 and passenger car No. 70 on a piece of the original rail line.

*Getting there:* on foot to City Hall at 1711 Miner Street and the path behind it. *Best for:* a short walk by the creek between the mill and Miner Street's shops.

## 04 · The Majestic Art Gallery

[The Majestic Art Gallery](/places/majestic-art-gallery/) at 1636 Miner Street is a cooperative of about 35 member artists, open daily 10 am–5 pm, with painting, photography, sculpture, pottery, jewelry and glass and new work each week; the City's visitor site puts it in the historic Faivre Building. [Citizens Park](/places/citizens-park/), at 1545 Miner Street on the way to the last stop, has a public restroom.

*Getting there:* on foot along Miner Street from City Hall. *Best for:* half an hour among local work, before the gallery closes at 5 pm.

## 05 · Beau Jo's Pizza

[Beau Jo's Pizza](/places/beau-jos-pizza/) at 1517 Miner Street calls itself the first Beau Jo's, serving its Colorado-style mountain pie since the 1970s: a hand-braided crust, finished with Colorado honey, plus salads, sandwiches and calzones with vegetarian and gluten-free options. It is open Sunday to Thursday 11 am–8 pm and Friday and Saturday 11 am–9 pm, with a weekday happy hour from 3 to 6 pm. Parties under 20 are seated first come, first served, and leashed dogs are welcome on the patio but not inside.

*Getting there:* on foot along Miner Street. *Best for:* a late lunch or an early dinner at the end of the walk.

## A simple first visit

1. Park downtown or in the Argo's lots, and start at the Heritage Museum at 2060 Miner Street once it opens.
2. Walk to the Argo on Riverside Drive for a mill tour, booked ahead; the last leaves at 3 pm.
3. Walk back to City Hall at 1711 Miner Street and take the path to the waterwheel.
4. Browse the Majestic before 5 pm; the restroom is in Citizens Park.
5. Mountain pie at Beau Jo's.

## Getting here

Drive times are estimates for normal conditions and vary with traffic, weather and construction; check a live routing service before you set out. The Mighty Argo gives about 33 miles and 35 minutes from Denver and about 20 miles and 25 minutes from Golden, and uses I-70 exit 241. In winter it points drivers to [COtrip](https://www.cotrip.org/), since Colorado's traction law applies on this stretch of I-70.

Park Idaho Springs says paid parking in town is in effect seven days a week from 10 am to 6 pm, starting at 50 cents for the first half hour, with a two-hour flat rate of $6; free 15-minute and 3-hour zones are located throughout downtown, and the Highway 103 lot costs $1 an hour to an $8 daily maximum. The Argo's own lots charge $2.95 for an hour up to $14.95 for the day at the gate, 20 percent less if booked at least the day before; the Argo says weekends, holidays and live music can fill them.

## Accessibility: what we can confirm

The Mighty Argo says its base terminal is a level, accessible two-minute walk from its lots, with marked accessible spaces closest to the doors, and that it has accessible restrooms at both terminals; the cable car takes guests with limited mobility, but the mill tour's steep terrain and historic architecture make it inaccessible to some, so call before booking. The City says the path to the waterwheel is paved and that Citizens Park has a public restroom.

Step-free entrances at the Heritage Museum, the Majestic and Beau Jo's, and the state of the sidewalks and curb ramps on Miner Street, have not been confirmed and are not claimed here; contact a business directly before relying on it.
