James Underhill, a Harvard graduate who came to Idaho Springs in 1897 as a consulting engineer and later taught mining at the Colorado School of Mines, built his office and home on Miner Street from 1912 with his wife, Lucy. The house has two front doors side by side: the east one into his office, the west one for Lucy's guests. Lucy deeded it to the Idaho Springs library in 1963 for use as a museum, hoping it would never become a tavern. The Historical Society of Idaho Springs now owns and maintains it, and says the home is exactly as the Underhills left it; the garden and courtyard behind it have been redone and can be rented for small events.

Read on 10 October 2026: the society's Underhill Museum page (history, the garden, the society's phone) and the City of Idaho Springs' visitor site, which lists the museum at 1414 Miner Street with the society's number but says the Underhills built 1416 Miner Street; the society's page says he bought the lot at 1413. Neither page gives opening hours. The handoff packet notes the same conflict and asks that no house number be guessed.
