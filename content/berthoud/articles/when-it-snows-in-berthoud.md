Shovel your sidewalk within 24 hours after the snow stops. That is the rule in the Town's Snow and Ice Removal Plan, which cites Chapter 15.12 of the Berthoud Municipal Code, and it covers the walk along your property: most sidewalks sit in the Town's right-of-way, and the plan makes the adjacent owner responsible for keeping them clear.

## What is yours

- **Your sidewalk**, no later than 24 hours after the end of the snow event.
- **Keep it out of the street.** The plan says not to put snow from a driveway or sidewalk onto the street.
- **Hydrants and mailboxes.** Clear the snow from around both.
- **Businesses** clear the sidewalk in front of their building. On Mountain Avenue the snow goes into the parking area in front of the business. A business that hires a contractor is responsible for where the snow is piled, so it does not block the neighbors or the Town's plows. Downtown, where some spots have no room to store snow and no direct sun to melt it, Public Works arranges with those businesses to move it.

## What the Town clears

The Streets Department plows and treats the municipal streets inside town limits. The Parks Department clears Town-owned parking lots, sidewalks and paved trails, also within 24 hours after the snow ends, and puts de-icer on the shaded stretches.

The Colorado Department of Transportation maintains most of Highway 56. The Town maintains the piece from US 287 east to the 1st Street roundabout.

The Town does not clear private streets, alleys, parking lots, sidewalks, trails or driveways. In many neighborhoods the trails, sidewalks and alleys belong to the homeowners' association or the metropolitan district, which is who to ask about them.

## Which streets get plowed, and when

The Town sorts its streets into three priorities, and how far down the list it goes depends on the storm.

| Storm | What the plan says happens |
|---|---|
| Under 3 inches | Usually ice melt only, and only on Priority 1 roads. Priority 2 and 3 roads are checked but usually not treated. |
| Over 3 inches | Plowing starts. Priority 1 roads are always cleared first; staff then assess Priority 2 and 3 roads and plow them if needed for safe travel. |
| Over 6 inches | Priority 1 roads are fully cleared first. Every Priority 2 and 3 road gets at least one lane, starting where the snow is deepest. |

- **Priority 1** is the major arterials, the roads that link the corners of town and reach police, fire and emergency medical services, plus school zones, roundabouts, steep hills and most intersections. These are treated in every snow and ice event.
- **Priority 2** is the collectors, the streets that connect the arterials to local streets. In most cases they are plowed only when more than 3 inches falls and the cold has kept it from melting.
- **Priority 3** is the local streets. The plan says sun and rising temperatures usually clear them while the crews are on the first two, and that in most cases they are maintained only in storms over 6 inches.

The Town's [Snow Removal Priority Map](https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/33b75518da964aef8ef2aa672901bce3/) shows which is which: Priority 1 in red, Priority 2 in blue, local streets uncolored.

## Leaves and street sweeping

The Town does not pick up leaf piles. Its Streets & Drainage page says piles made by homeowners will not be picked up, that sweepers cannot take branches or large piles, that leaves on your yard are yours to clean up, and that it is illegal to blow leaves or grass clippings into the street.

Street sweeping runs in the second week of each month, 7 am to 3 pm on weekdays, with extra passes in the heavy-leaf months. The main season is April 1 through November 30; sweepers keep working in winter when it is above freezing. That week, move cars, trash cans and basketball hoops off the street.
