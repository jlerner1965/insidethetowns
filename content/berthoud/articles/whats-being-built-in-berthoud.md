Berthoud counted 5,105 people at the 2010 census and 10,332 at the 2020 one, and the building has not stopped. The Town's community development director told the [Berthoud Weekly Surveyor](https://berthoudsurveyor.com/berthoud-growth-part-1-the-towns-planning-and-approval-process/) in March 2026 that the Town had issued 3,125 new-home building permits over nine years, that permits had slowed over the past two, and that builders were still submitting proposals. This page is the list of those proposals: every sizable project on the Town's own [Development Activity Map](https://berthoud.org/180/Current-Development-Review-Projects), which its planners last edited on October 15, 2026, with what the Town's ordinances and the Surveyor's reporting add. It is updated as projects move.

## How a project moves

The Town's map defines the steps, and the Surveyor's [March 2026 explainer](https://berthoudsurveyor.com/berthoud-growth-part-1-the-towns-planning-and-approval-process/) walks through them. Land outside town limits is first **annexed**, which the Board of Trustees may grant or refuse at its discretion, and **zoned** at the same time. A **neighborhood master plan** is the concept: where the homes, streets, parks and open space go. A **preliminary plat** adds the engineering; a **final plat** completes the subdivision and is what lots are sold from. Then come building permits. Annexations, zonings, master plans and preliminary plats are heard in public by the [Planning Commission](/events/planning-commission-2026-11-12/), which recommends, and then by the Board of Trustees, which decides. A **site plan** for a single commercial or multi-family building is approved by staff without a hearing. Before any hearing the Town notifies the property owners around the site, posts a sign on it, and publishes the date in the newspaper.

## Denied or withdrawn

- **Tundra Flats.** A 6.6-acre site on the north side of Mountain Avenue between Meadowlark Drive and Gateway Park Boulevard, where Golden Eye Investment proposed five buildings: one mixed-use, with 3,700 square feet of ground-floor commercial and two floors of apartments above, and four residential buildings, 140 to 150 units in all. Town staff found it complied with the Comprehensive Plan. The Planning Commission voted 3–2 against it on May 28, 2026, the majority wanting more commercial space on a site the plan reserves for it; the developer said a CDOT easement had killed the drive-through that Dairy Queen wanted. The Board denied the rezoning from planned unit development to neighbourhood commercial on July 28 (Ordinance 1387), and BizWest reported on August 14 that the owner had sued the Board over the vote.
- **Revere.** The 38 acres at the northwest corner of US 287 and Mountain Avenue, where voters rescinded the Board's rezoning and capped the density on April 7, 2026. [The full account is its own page.](/articles/revere-property-what-the-april-vote-decided/)
- **Farmstead 4th Filing**, nine acres of commercial lots, was withdrawn by the owner, and a **Dollar General** at 3410 North Berthoud Parkway was withdrawn by the applicant.

## Still unresolved

- **Campion (Broadmeadow).** The True Life Companies of Denver proposed annexing 63 acres southeast of Campion Academy, between the railroad and US 287, for 198 homes: 128 single-family and 70 duplexes. Campion residents opposed it at a December 2024 meeting and since. The Board accepted the petition for review 5–0 on May 27, 2025, and the hearing was moved to September 9, 2025. The project is not on the Town's current map.
- **Turion, at I-25 and Highway 56.** More than 2,000 acres in Berthoud and Mead, between I-25 and Weld County Road 11, planned in 2022 for more than 4,000 homes and 5.3 million square feet of commercial space. The Town's June 22, 2026 staff memo says meetings continue with the Turion developers on agreements and infrastructure for delivering water to I-25 and Highway 56.

## The rules that changed this year

- **Data centers.** Ordinance 1391, passed in September 2026, suspends applications for large data centers for a year while the Town writes rules for them. KFKA reported on October 27 that staff were to brief the Board on the issues raised at a September 29 study session, likely a definition of data centers and size as a factor in permitting.
- **The Comprehensive Plan.** The Board added a water conservation element on April 14, 2026 (Ordinance 1379), to meet a 2024 state law tied to affordable-housing grants, and has since adopted a Strategic Growth Element on infill and redevelopment, which the Town's planning page lists beside the Housing Diversity Plan and Water Supply Element.
- **Maps and code.** The Town's zoning map was updated on April 6, 2026 and its annexation map on April 14. Supplement 19 to the Municipal Code, with changes to Chapter 30, the Development Code, was published in July.
- **Boundaries with the neighbors.** The Board approved an agreement with Mead defining growth boundaries in June 2025, and in July 2026 took up an updated growth-management agreement with Larimer County.

## How to follow it

The Planning Commission meets on the second and fourth Thursdays at 6 pm and the Board of Trustees on the second and fourth Tuesdays at 6:30 pm, both at [Town Hall](/places/berthoud-town-hall/), 807 Mountain Avenue; agendas and packets are on the Town's [CivicClerk portal](https://berthoudco.portal.civicclerk.com/). The Town's [map](https://berthoud.org/180/Current-Development-Review-Projects) names the planner for each project, and the Planning Department answers at 970-344-5826. Annexations and rezonings are quasi-judicial, which is why the Board does not take comment on a petition until the public hearing; that hearing is where to speak.
