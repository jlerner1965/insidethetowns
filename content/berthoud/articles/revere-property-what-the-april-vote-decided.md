The Revere property is 38 acres of open ground at the northwest corner of US 287 and Mountain Avenue, the west entrance to town, across the highway from Westside Crossing. For years it carried planned-unit-development zoning, the kind that ties a parcel to a specific plan. In 2025 the owner asked the Town to change that, and in April 2026 the town's voters answered. This is what happened, in order, without an argument for either side.

## What the Board approved

On October 14, 2025 the Board of Trustees passed Ordinance 1363, which rezoned the property from planned unit development to the Town's suburban commercial and suburban residential districts. The neighborhood master plan that went with it, as the [Surveyor](https://berthoudsurveyor.com/berthoud-voters-to-decide-revere-zoning-and-density/) summarized it, called for 81 single-family lots and two commercial lots totaling 5.4 acres. The developer is Jeff Mark of The Landhuis Company. It followed an earlier proposal for the site in 2023 and 2024.

## The two petitions

Berthoud's rules let residents refer an ordinance to the voters by petition within 30 days of its passage, with signatures from five percent of registered voters, which in 2026 meant 565. A referendum petition on Ordinance 1363 was filed with 589 valid signatures, which put the ordinance on hold. A second, separate petition proposed an ordinance of its own, limiting residential density on the same parcel, and gathered 640 valid signatures. In January the Board sent both to the April 7 municipal election.

So the ballot asked two questions about one piece of land:

- **Question 1A** asked whether Ordinance 1363, the rezoning, should take effect. A yes vote kept the Board's decision; a no vote rescinded it.
- **Question 3A** asked whether the Town should adopt an initiated ordinance limiting gross residential density to one unit per 2.29 acres within Lot 1 of the Smith Subdivision, the legal description of the Revere land. On 38 acres that works out to about 16 homes.

Both sides wrote to the Surveyor. A [letter for 1A](https://berthoudsurveyor.com/280444-2/) argued that 81 homes on 38 acres, about 2.8 to the acre, was lower than the Town's land-use plan permitted and had been through the Planning Commission and the Board as the process intends. A [letter against it](https://berthoudsurveyor.com/letter-to-the-editor-protect-berthouds-character-vote-no-on-1a-yes-on-3a/), signed by the petition's organizers, argued that the rezoning shifted a site the plan meant to be primarily commercial to primarily residential, and that on the buildable part of the parcel the density was closer to 7.8 homes an acre.

## How the town voted

The Town's certified count for April 7, 2026 ([abstract of votes cast](https://www.berthoud.org/DocumentCenter/View/7436/April-7-2026-Municipal-Election-Results), 3,793 ballots):

| Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| 1A: let Ordinance 1363 take effect | 1,144 | 2,636 |
| 3A: cap the land at one home per 2.29 acres | 2,585 | 1,192 |

Each passed or failed by more than two to one. The same ballot filled four trustee seats from four candidates: Casey Grace (2,065 votes), Sean Murphy (1,940) and Karen Anderson (1,878) for four-year terms, and Arno Olbricht (1,292) for a two-year term.

## What the vote means

With Ordinance 1363 rescinded, the property's zoning goes back to planned unit development, which the Surveyor notes the Town's current land-use code no longer prefers: the code is written for single-use districts. On top of that sits the 3A ordinance, which the Town must now apply to any residential plan for the parcel. The two together do not say what can be built; they say what cannot.

After the vote the developer told the Surveyor, "I don't have a game plan yet," that he would consult his attorney, and that it "may be a redesign or legal." Bridget Guccione, the petition's administrative agent, said the group was "not opposed to development" and wanted it to be "smart," pointing to the earlier proposal as a model. The Town attorney was reviewing the development proposal and the results, and on May 12 staff told the Board that implementation work was under way. No new application for the site is on the Town's development map.

## Where to watch

Any new plan for the parcel would come first to the [Planning Commission](/events/planning-commission-2026-12-10/), second and fourth Thursdays at 6 pm, and then to the Board, second and fourth Tuesdays at 6:30 pm, both at [Town Hall](/places/berthoud-town-hall/). Agendas post to the Town's [CivicClerk portal](https://berthoudco.portal.civicclerk.com/).
