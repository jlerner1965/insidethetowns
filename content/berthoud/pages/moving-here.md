## What you are moving to

Berthoud is a statutory town of about 10,300 people (2020 census) on the Larimer–Weld county line, 21 miles south of Fort Collins and 43 north of Denver, on the bluff above the Little Thompson River. It calls itself the Garden Spot of Colorado, a name earned by the irrigated farms around it, and as recently as the 2010 census it was a farm town of about five thousand with a five-block Main Street. It has roughly doubled since 2010: new subdivisions on every side, a reservoir park opened in 2020, a $32 million recreation center in 2021, a bike park in 2023, and the [downtown](/places/downtown-mural-walk/) filling up with breweries, galleries and antique malls. The old grid of brick storefronts and converted houses along Mountain Avenue is still the center of it.

## Housing

Mostly single-family homes: the older grid around Mountain Avenue and the 7th Street historic neighborhood, ranchettes on the county roads, and the large newer subdivisions (Heron Lakes and TPC Colorado around Lonetree Reservoir, Berthoud Harvest, Prairiestar, Hammond Farm and others) that account for most of what is on the market. More are on the way: [what's being built in Berthoud](/articles/whats-being-built-in-berthoud/) lists every project on the Town's development map, from the 439 lots of Legacy Park's first filing to the Mountain View annexation of up to 618 homes, and where each one stands. The 2020 census counted 3,879 households; the median household income was $119,385 in the Census Bureau's [2020–2024 American Community Survey](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDP5Y2024.DP03?g=160XX00US0806255), an estimate rather than a count. The listings sites linked in the sidebar show what is for sale today. Most of the new subdivisions have metropolitan districts and homeowners' associations with their own fees; ask for the mill levy before you fall for the house.

## Schools

Berthoud is in the [Thompson School District R2-J](https://www.tsd.org/), based in Loveland. All four public schools are in town: Berthoud Elementary, Ivy Stockwell Elementary, Turner Middle School and Berthoud High School, whose Spartans play at the Bein complex on the south side. The district's site has boundaries, enrollment windows and bus routes.

## Getting around

US 287 has skirted the west side of town since the 2006 bypass, which is why downtown is quiet and why it is fifteen minutes to Loveland or Longmont on the highway. I-25 is ten minutes east on Colorado 56, then about fifty minutes to Denver and an hour to Denver International Airport; Fort Collins is 25 minutes north. For transit, the [FLEX](https://www.ridetransfort.com/) regional bus runs between Fort Collins, Loveland, Berthoud, Longmont and Boulder, and the Town's [Berthoud Area Transportation Service](https://berthoud.org/159/Berthoud-Area-Transportation-Service-BAT) (BATS) gives door-to-door rides in town and to Loveland; for Longmont, [RAFT](https://berthoudraft.org/) carries riders 60 and over and adults with a disability. Downtown is walkable; the rest of town is a car or a bike.

## Utilities, trash, fire, police

- **Water and sewer** are Town of Berthoud utilities, billed by [Town Hall](/places/berthoud-town-hall/) at 807 Mountain Avenue (970-532-2643).
- **Electricity** is Xcel Energy in most of town and Poudre Valley REA on parts of the edges; the provider depends on the address.
- **Natural gas** is Xcel Energy.
- **Trash and recycling** are private: residents choose from the haulers licensed by the Town, listed in its [business directory](https://www.berthoud.org/1387/Trash-and-Utility-Providers), and some subdivisions contract a hauler through the HOA.
- **Police** is the [Larimer County Sheriff's Berthoud Squad](https://www.larimer.gov/sheriff/patrol/berthoud-squad), under contract; non-emergency 970-416-1985.
- **Fire and emergency medical** response is the [Berthoud Fire Protection District](https://www.berthoudfire.org/), a separate district covering 103 square miles from its station at 248 Welch Avenue; non-emergency 970-532-2264.
- **Building permits, planning and zoning** are Town departments; the [Planning Commission](/events/?category=civic) meets on the second and fourth Thursdays.

## Government

A mayor and six trustees, elected at large, make up the Board of Trustees, which meets on the [second and fourth Tuesdays](/events/?category=civic) at 6:30 pm at Town Hall. A Planning Commission, a Parks, Open Space, Recreation and Trails committee, a Tree Advisory Committee, a Historic Preservation Advisory Committee and a Youth Advisory Commission are filled by application. Residents can send an ordinance to the voters by petition within 30 days of its passage, with signatures from five percent of registered voters, and in April 2026 they did: the town overturned the Board's rezoning of the 38-acre Revere property at US 287 and Mountain Avenue and capped it at one home per 2.29 acres ([what the April vote decided](/articles/revere-property-what-the-april-vote-decided/)). In 2019 the Board barred pet shops in town from selling dogs or cats that do not come from a shelter or rescue (Ordinance 1267).

## Living here

Summer is the Saturday [market](/events/) in Town Park, the water feature and the pool, concerts in the Pioneer Museum courtyard, and Carter Lake's swim beach ten minutes up the road. Fall is Oktoberfest in Town Park on the first Saturday of October, Trick or Treat Street on Mountain Avenue, and the Zombie Fun Run. Winter is A Very Merry Berthoud and the Parade of Lights on the first Saturday of December, the craft fair at the rec center, and the hill behind the high school when it snows. Year-round there is trivia, a run club and live music at [City Star](/places/city-star-brewing/), a [bike park](/places/berthoud-bike-park/) that is free, and a [reservoir loop](/places/berthoud-reservoir-loop/) for the evening walk.
