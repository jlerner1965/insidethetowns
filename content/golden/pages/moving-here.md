## Who lives here

Golden is a home-rule city in Jefferson County, governed under its own City Charter. An elected City Council and Mayor appoint the City Manager, City Attorney and Municipal Judge ([City Leadership](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/government/city_leadership/index.php)). The Council has seven members: four elected from wards, two from districts and the mayor at large, and it meets twice a month in Council Chambers at City Hall, 911 10th Street ([City Council](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/government/city_leadership/city_council.php)). The City is consolidating its offices into a new city hall and police building east of Washington Avenue, which is under construction ([Heart of Golden](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/government/initiatives_plans/city-wide_plans___initiatives/index.php)).

Jefferson County's government is based here too: the main county campus is at 100 Jefferson County Parkway, Golden ([Jefferson County](https://www.jeffco.us/2051/Driving-Directions-and-Parking)). Golden City was established in 1859 as a supply center for miners and settlers, and the Colorado School of Mines, a public R1 research university in applied science and engineering, has been here since the 1870s: it became a territorial institution in 1874 and a state institution in 1876 ([Mines history](https://www.mines.edu/about/history-and-traditions/); [About Mines](https://www.mines.edu/about/)).

The 2020 Census counted 20,399 people ([2020 Census, in the State Demography Office's census file](https://storage.googleapis.com/co-publicdata/historical-census.csv)). The City's tourism office, Visit Golden, puts the city's area at 8 square miles, 12 miles west of Denver ([Golden at a Glance](https://www.visitgolden.com/blog/post/golden-at-a-glance/)). One thing to know early: the City notes that not all Golden mailing addresses are within city limits ([New Resident Guide](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/new_resident_guide.php)), so check an address before assuming City services and City rules apply.

## Housing

Golden's oldest neighborhoods date from the 1859 town plat. The City's neighborhood plans describe them: West Downtown (the 12th Street and College Hill area), North Clear Creek (the 8th and 9th Street neighborhood, Goosetown and the CoorsTek area), Central Golden south of downtown along Jackson and Ford streets, the North Neighborhoods north of State Highway 58, Golden Heights and Golden Hills east of I-70 and south of US 6, the South Neighborhoods south of 6th Avenue (Heritage Hills, Tripp Ranch, Eagle Ridge, Golden Terrace, Heritage Square and Golden Ridge), and the South Golden Road neighborhoods at the city's eastern edge ([Neighborhood Plans](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/government/initiatives_plans/neighborhood_plans/index.php)). Three districts are locally designated historic districts, 8th and 9th Street, 12th Street and East Street, and most exterior changes to a building in one of them need a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Historic Preservation Board ([Historic Property Preservation](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/business/land_use_development/historic_property_preservation.php)).

The City lists its income-restricted housing: Flats at Ford, Canyon Gate and Lewis Court (Foothills Regional Housing) and Golden Pointe Apartments, plus two mobile home parks, Golden Hills and Golden Terrace, which it says have historically offered over 500 homes. In February 2023 the Golden Hills Resident Cooperative bought its park with a $2 million forgivable loan from the City and Jefferson County. The City has opted in to Proposition 123, committing to add 78 affordable units by 2026 ([Housing Policies](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/housing/affordable_housing.php)). Accessory dwelling units are allowed with single-household detached homes in the R-E, R-1, R-1A, R-2 and R-3 zones if the owner lives on the property, provides one parking space and records a deed restriction; no more than three people may occupy one ([ADUs](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/business/building_construction/accessory_dwelling_units.php)). A short-term rental in a residential zone must be the owner's home for at least ten months of the year, and a licence costs $200 for two years ([Short-Term Rentals](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/business/land_use_development/short_term_rentals.php)).

No prices here: the only dated government source is the Jefferson County Assessor, whose 2025 revaluation set values as of 30 June 2024 from sales between 1 July 2022 and 30 June 2024, in effect for tax years 2025 and 2026. Its Property Records Search shows any address's valuation and sales history ([Assessor](https://www.jeffco.us/4593/20252026-Property-Valuation-and-Appeal-I)). The listings in the sidebar show what is for sale.

## Schools

Golden is in Jeffco Public Schools, which divides the county into 17 articulation areas. The Golden area has five elementary schools, Kyffin, Mitchell, Ralston, Shelton and Welchester, plus Bell Middle School and Golden High School ([Golden Articulation Area](https://golden.jeffcopublicschools.org/about/articulation-area)). Schools are assigned by address, and the district's boundary maps and School Finder show which serves a given house; Jeffco also offers choice enrollment, so any student in the county can attend any Jeffco school with space. Compass Montessori – Golden is among the district's charter schools ([Articulation Areas & Maps](https://www.jeffcopublicschools.org/about/articulation-areas)).

## Getting around

By road, Golden is US 6, Colorado 93 and I-70. Jefferson County's own directions have US 6 (West 6th Avenue) running about 11 miles from downtown Denver to Johnson Road at the county campus, Colorado 93 coming south and becoming US 6, and I-70 reached at exits 260 and 262 ([Jefferson County](https://www.jeffco.us/2051/Driving-Directions-and-Parking)). West of town US 6 runs 11.37 miles through Clear Creek Canyon between the junction with Colorado 58 and 93 and Colorado 119; CDOT resurfaced it, widened shoulders and replaced the guardrail in a project finished in 2023 ([CDOT](https://www.codot.gov/projects/archives/us6-clear-creek-canyon-resurfacing)). The City says congestion grows as Golden grows, and points drivers to its road-closure map and to CDOT's [COtrip](https://www.cotrip.org/) for the state highways ([Drive](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/getting_around/drive.php)). As a foothills community, Golden plows all city streets in a storm, primary roads first, then secondary, then residential, over more than 241 lane miles ([Street Maintenance](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/services/street_maintenance.php)).

Golden is the end of RTD's W Line light rail, which runs from Union Station to Jefferson County Government Center–Golden Station ([RTD rail](https://www.rtd-denver.com/routes-services/rail)); the County says the line opened in April 2013 and the station is just outside its administration building ([Jefferson County](https://www.jeffco.us/2051/Driving-Directions-and-Parking)), and the City says trains run every half hour with stops in Lakewood and Denver; RTD bus 17 links 10th & Washington downtown to Federal Center Station daily ([Ride](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/getting_around/ride.php)). Golden FlexRide, RTD's on-demand service, covers most of the city Monday to Friday 5:30 am to 7 pm and Saturday 8 am to 7 pm, with no Sunday service; the Golden Heights and Golden Hills neighborhoods and the Coors Technology Center are outside its area ([Golden FlexRide](https://www.rtd-denver.com/routes-services/other-services/flexride/flexride-service-areas/golden)). Fares are $2.75 standard and $1.35 discounted, and riders 19 and under ride free ([FlexRide](https://www.rtd-denver.com/routes-services/other-services/flexride)). The City runs the Ore Cart with Colorado School of Mines, a free shuttle on three routes that link downtown, the Mines campus and the W Line station, with no service on Mines holidays ([Ore Cart](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/services/ore_cart/index.php)). CDOT's Bustang does not stop in Golden; the nearest stop is Federal Center Station in Lakewood ([Ride](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/getting_around/ride.php)). The City counts 19 parks and 42 miles of paved trails ([Parks & Trails](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/parks_trails/index.php)).

## Services

- **Water and sewer** are the City of Golden. Drinking water comes from Clear Creek and is treated at the City's plant ([Water Treatment Plant](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/services/water_quality_protection/water_treatment_plant.php)). Water and wastewater rates rose 6.2 percent on 1 January 2026, partly to pay for required upgrades at the Molson Coors Wastewater Treatment Plant, where Golden's wastewater is processed. A home with a 5/8-inch meter pays a $13.23 monthly service charge plus $6.44 to $9.65 per 1,000 gallons by tier; wastewater is $8.99 a month plus $3.86 per 1,000 gallons. Movers use the Start, Stop or Transfer form, there is a $25 charge for a final bill, and the title company usually handles the transfer in a sale; Utility Billing is 303-384-8026 ([Water Service](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/services/water_service.php)).
- **Trash, recycling and compost** are a City contract with 5280 Waste Solutions (303-277-8727) on a pay-as-you-throw basis: $9.24 a month for a 32-gallon cart, $18.16 for 64 gallons and $30.06 for 96 gallons, with recycling and organics carts included. Carts go out by 7 am ([Trash, Recycling & Compost](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/services/trash_recycling_compost/index.php)).
- **Electricity** is Xcel Energy, whose Public Safety Power Shutoff in December 2025 spanned several days and affected much of the city; the City sent Xcel a formal letter of concern on 2 January 2026 ([City news](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/news_detail_T18_R143.php)). The City offers rebates for solar and battery backup ([Energy](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/sustainable_golden/energy.php)). The City's pages do not name the natural gas provider.
- **Fire** is the Golden Fire Department, a City department of staff and volunteers founded in 1879, with an ISO rating of 2; every firefighter is also certified for wildland fire. After hours call 303-980-7300; administration is 303-384-8094 ([Fire Department](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/services/fire_department/index.php); [About Us](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/services/fire_department/about_us.php)).
- **Police** is the Golden Police Department at 911 10th Street, 303-384-8045. Non-emergency dispatch is Jeffcom, 303-980-7300 ([Police contact](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/police/contact_us.php)).
- **Library** is Jefferson County Public Library's Golden Library at 1019 10th Street, opened in 1996 next to the Golden History Museum on the Clear Creek trail; open 9 to 8 Monday to Thursday, 9 to 5 Friday and Saturday, noon to 5 Sunday ([Golden Library](https://jeffcolibrary.org/locations/GN/)).
- **Recreation** is the City's Parks and Recreation department. The Golden Community Center at 1470 10th Street has indoor leisure and lap pools, a hot tub, a gym, an indoor running track and a licensed preschool, and opens at 5:30 am on weekdays ([Golden Community Center](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/golden_community_center/index.php)). The New Resident Guide adds The Splash outdoor water park and Fossil Trace Golf Club ([New Resident Guide](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/new_resident_guide.php)). Jefferson County Open Space, headquartered in Golden, manages 27 parks with more than 58,000 acres and 275 trail miles ([Jeffco Open Space](https://www.jeffco.us/4867/Open-Space)). The City's Golden History Museum at 923 10th Street is free and open Tuesday to Saturday ([Golden History Museum](https://www.goldenhistory.org/)).
- **Emergency alerts** come through LookoutAlert, Jefferson County's notification system ([Emergency Preparedness](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/health_safety/emergency_preparedness.php)).

## Before you decide

- **Wildfire.** The City says its location along the foothills and next to open space makes wildfire risk part of living here. Golden adopted a Community Wildfire Protection Plan in 2007, updated it in 2022 and plans another update in late 2026, and in 2026 adopted the Golden Wildfire Resiliency Code, with building standards for higher-risk areas ([Wildfire Ready](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/wildfire_ready/index.php)). Homes on the edges of town are in the wildland-urban interface ([Prepare Your Home](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/wildfire_ready/prepare_home.php)). The City also warns that power outages can come from wildfire-mitigation shutoffs ([Energy](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/sustainable_golden/energy.php)).
- **City limits.** Check whether an address is inside the city; the City's short-term rental page points to the county map, with the city boundary in blue ([Short-Term Rentals](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/business/land_use_development/short_term_rentals.php)). Outside the line, Jefferson County's land use code applies, and the County is rewriting it for unincorporated areas in 2026 ([Jefferson County](https://www.jeffco.us/)).
- **Old houses.** Homes built before 1940 are the most likely to have lead or galvanized water service lines; the City is inventorying them and some owners qualify for free replacement ([Housing Policies](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/housing/affordable_housing.php)). In a historic district, exterior work needs the Preservation Board's approval ([Historic Property Preservation](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/business/land_use_development/historic_property_preservation.php)).
- **Bears and snow.** The City reports increased bear activity in drought and asks residents to put carts out the morning of collection ([Trash](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/services/trash_recycling_compost/index.php)). Every city street gets plowed, but residential streets come last ([Street Maintenance](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/services/street_maintenance.php)).
- **Clear Creek and downtown.** Summer tubing visitors to Clear Creek have increased dramatically, the City says ([Clear Creek Management](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/government/initiatives_plans/city-wide_plans___initiatives/index.php)), and downtown parking management has changed in the past year ([Getting Around](https://www.cityofgolden.gov/community/getting_around/index.php)).
