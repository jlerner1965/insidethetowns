Frederick has 41 metropolitan districts on the Town's list. Whether your house sits in one changes your property-tax bill. Here is how to check, what the district and the seller now have to tell you, and where to swim.

## What a metro district is

The Town's [Metropolitan Districts page](https://www.frederickco.gov/838/Metropolitan-Districts) calls one "a special district with limited governmental powers", set up under Colorado's special district law. Most are formed to pay for the roads, pipes, drainage and parks a new subdivision needs, and they can own and maintain those improvements afterwards. SilverStone Metropolitan District No. 1's [2022 budget](https://silverstonemetrodistricts.specialdistrict.org/files/293404575/2022+Budget+Message+-+Silverstone+Metro+District+No.+1.pdf) is a plain example: it was formed to finance storm drainage, water, sewer, utilities, streets, traffic controls and parks for Silverstone, with a $50 million debt limit shared by its three districts. The [Silverstone districts' site](https://silverstonemetrodistricts.specialdistrict.org/) says they repay that debt through property taxes. That is the part homeowners feel: a metro district's mill levy is on your tax bill alongside the Town's, the county's and the rest.

An HOA is a separate thing. It enforces covenants and collects dues, and it does not tax.

## Is your address in one?

- **The county's list.** Look the address up on Weld County's [property portal](https://apps.weld.gov/propertyportal/) and open the "Taxing Districts" box. The "Tax Authorities" list there names every district that taxes the property. The Carbon Valley Parks and Recreation District tells residents to use the same box to confirm they pay "Carbon Valley Rec" ([district residency](https://www.cvprd.com/district-residency)), and a metro district shows up the same way.
- **Your tax statement.** Each district that taxes you is a line on it.
- **The Town's map.** The [Metropolitan Districts page](https://www.frederickco.gov/838/Metropolitan-Districts) has an interactive map of the districts and links each one to its filings with the State Auditor.

## The districts on the Town's list

Carriage Hills; Clearview Villages; Frederick; Gateway to Frederick Nos. 1 to 6; Godding Hollow; Hidden Creek; Maple Ridge; Marketplace; Prosperity; Shores on Plum Creek Nos. 1 to 10; Silverstone Nos. 1 to 3; the Village East Community Metropolitan District and Village East Nos. 1 to 5; Westview; Wildflower Nos. 1 to 3; and Wyndham Hill Nos. 1 to 4.

Each name on the Town's page opens the district's record on the [State Auditor's local government portal](https://apps.leg.co.gov/osa/lg/local_govs/1233), with its mailing address and every audit or audit exemption it has filed. Numbered districts with one name belong to one development, as Silverstone's three share one debt limit.

The **Frederick Metropolitan District** has the Town's name, but it is one district among the 41. A Weld County court declared it organized on November 18, 2019. Its [page on the Town's site](https://www.frederickco.gov/1162/Frederick-Metropolitan-District) lists a seven-seat board, one seat vacant.

## What the district and the seller now have to tell you

Under [HB25-1219](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb25-1219), signed on May 29, 2025 and in force since August 6, 2025, a metro district with a website has to post its annual meeting notice on the home page, along with board and manager contacts, meeting dates, the call for board nominations, the other governments that overlap it, and a contact for service problems. It also has to explain what a metro district is and how a resident can serve on its board.

The same law covers home sales. Anyone selling a house inside a metro district must give the buyer the district's annual notice and service plan, its borrowing, tax and fee powers, an estimate of the district's property tax for the year of the sale, and a recent tax certificate or statement showing the other levies. The seller must also include a written statement that district actions may raise residents' costs. If you are buying, ask for that packet early.

## Pools and clubhouses

Whether your neighborhood has a pool or clubhouse, and who runs it, is a question for your HOA. **Prairie Greens**, the YES! Communities neighborhood at 7781 Mountain View Drive, lists a clubhouse, fitness center, swimming pool, playground and picnic area among its amenities ([YES! Communities](https://www.yescommunities.com/About-Prairie-Greens)).

For everyone else, the pool is the rec center's: the **Carbon Valley Recreation Center**, 701 5th Street, has a lap pool and an activity pool with a lazy river and slide ([pool schedule](https://www.cvprd.com/pool-schedule)). Anyone can swim. If your address pays "Carbon Valley Rec", you pay district rates.

## Before you buy

1. Look the address up on the county's property portal and read the Tax Authorities list.
2. Ask the seller for the metro district disclosure the 2025 law requires.
3. Ask the HOA, separately, what its dues cover and whether the neighborhood has a pool or clubhouse, and who runs it.
4. Find the district on the Town's list and read its latest budget and audit on the State Auditor's portal or the district's own site.
