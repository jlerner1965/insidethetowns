Loveland has put sculpture in its parks since 1985, the year a group that had staged an outdoor sculpture show the summer before became the Loveland High Plains Arts Council and the City became, by its own account, the first in Colorado to pass an Art in Public Places ordinance. Four decades on there are two large free sculpture parks, one on the north side of town and one at Centerra, and a City collection of several hundred pieces spread through downtown, the Civic Center and the neighbourhoods. All of it can be seen on foot and none of it costs anything except one weekend in August. Here is how it fits together.

## Benson Sculpture Garden

[Benson Sculpture Garden](/places/benson-sculpture-garden/) is at 2908 Aspen Drive, at the corner of West 29th Street and Beech Drive across from Loveland High School. The [arts council](https://www.sculptureinthepark.org/about-benson), which has donated every piece in it to the City, describes 10 acres of paved, accessible paths, ponds and lawn with 188 sculptures on permanent display, valued at more than $5 million; its [FAQ](https://www.sculptureinthepark.org/faq) says 190, and the City's public art site says 178. The count moves because pieces are added most years: six went in during June 2022 alone, bought with the proceeds of the 2021 show. In 2015 the garden crossed 29th Street into North Lake Park with three new pieces, and that section now holds more than 15.

The garden is open all year at any hour, and admission is free apart from the show weekend. The flush restrooms are open 7 am–9 pm. There are benches at the gazebo and around the park but no picnic tables. The arts council's FAQ says parking, including accessible spaces, is along Aspen Drive and the surrounding streets. Dogs are allowed on a leash under the City's park rules, except during the show. The gazebo holds 50 and is reserved through the City's Parks and Recreation office at 500 East Third Street, Suite 200, weekdays 8 am–4:30 pm, 970-962-2727, or online; no tables are provided with it. Groups of ten or more can ask for one of a limited number of free tours at 970-663-2940, and the council's map and artist cross-reference guide can be downloaded from its site for a walk on your own.

## Sculpture in the Park

The garden exists for the council's annual show and sale, held on the second weekend of August, the one time a ticket is needed to walk in. The south-west corner of the park closes from August 1 to 16 each year for setup and takedown. The [43rd Sculpture in the Park](https://www.sculptureinthepark.org/) is set for August 13–15, 2027: the Friday is the Patron Party, a 21-and-over evening with cocktail attire and $100 tickets, and the public days are Saturday August 14 and Sunday August 15, with [general admission](https://www.sculptureinthepark.org/ticket-information) $10 at the gate and children 14 and under free. Tickets go on sale January 1, 2027. Artists' applications for 2027 are expected to open between mid-October and early November 2026. The council's office is at 125 East 7th Street downtown, open Monday to Thursday 8:30 am–1:30 pm.

## Chapungu Sculpture Park at Centerra

[Chapungu](/places/chapungu-sculpture-park/) is on the other side of town, just east of the Promenade Shops at Centerra off US 34 and Centerra Parkway. It opened in 2007 on land donated by Centerra's master developer, Realberry, then called McWhinney, and shows [82 monumental stone sculptures](https://chapunguatcenterra.com/) hand-carved by Zimbabwean artists, arranged over 26 acres under eight themes: Nature and the Environment, Village Life, the Role of Women, the Elders, the Spirit World, Custom and Legend, the Family and the Children. The [name](https://chapunguatcenterra.com/about/) is the Shona word for the bateleur eagle, which the park's text calls a spirit messenger and a good omen, and the sculptures come from the Chapungu workshop community that has promoted the art form since 1970.

Admission is free and the park is open daily 6 am–10 pm, all year. The [amenities page](https://chapunguatcenterra.com/amenities/) lists 1.5 miles of concrete path and fine-gravel trail following the Greeley and Loveland Irrigation Canal, two bridges, six access paths in from the Promenade Shops, benches, interpretive and sculpture identification signs, a public restroom in the summer months, and a pet water fountain; dogs are welcome and the whole park is wheelchair accessible. The planting is 2,900 native shrubs and trees and 7,000 ornamental grasses. Centerra's summer concert series runs from mid-July to mid-August on the Great Lawn, which can also be booked for events.

## The City's collection and the downtown pieces

The City's [Art in Public Places](https://www.lovelandpublicart.org/) program sits inside its Cultural Services Division. The 1985 ordinance puts one percent of every City capital project worth $50,000 or more toward buying, showing and maintaining art, and a nine-member Visual Arts Commission appointed by the City Council decides what is acquired, accepted and placed; it meets on the first Thursday of each month at 5 pm at the Loveland Museum, 503 North Lincoln Avenue, and its agendas and minutes are posted. The [collection](https://www.lovelandpublicart.org/the-collection/) now runs to 581 works valued at about $12.9 million, more than two-thirds of that value donated, the arts council being the largest donor. The pieces go into the right-of-way, parks, infrastructure and City buildings, which in practice means benches, bike racks, bridges and ponds as well as plinths.

For the downtown part, the City's [Otocast tour](https://www.lovelandpublicart.org/otocast-public-art-tour/) is a free self-guided walk through the Creative District: download the Otocast app, search for Loveland, CO, and it gives audio interviews with the artists, photographs and directions to each stop, with the [Rialto Theater](/places/rialto-theater-center/) and the Loveland Museum as detours. The City's [sculpture page](https://www.lovelandpublicart.org/sculpture/) fronts its Civic Center and downtown group with George Lundeen's bronze Joy of Music from 1989, and the pieces pictured across its public art pages include Mark Aeling's stainless-steel Acumen from 2020, Melanie Yazzie's powder-coated steel Two Minds Meeting from 2020, and Amelia Furman's mural The Story of Our Rialto, bought in 2021. The same page names a McWhinney-Hahn Sculpture Park without giving details. Indoors, the program's TAAP 2D exhibit of regional painting and photography hangs in the Loveland Public Library's Central Galleria, this year with poems by local poets beside the works; the 2026–2027 selection is up through February 2027.

The City's own lovgov.org pages, including its Benson Sculpture Garden listing, refused every request the day this was checked, so the City's restroom hours and reservation rules for the park are not repeated here; the arts council's figures above are what could be verified. Questions about the collection go to the Public Art Manager, Suzanne Janssen, at 970-962-2495.
