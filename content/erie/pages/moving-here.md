## What you are moving to

Erie is a home-rule town of about 30,000 people (2020 census) on the Boulder–Weld county line, twenty minutes from Boulder and thirty-five from Denver and its airport, on the plateau between Coal Creek and I-25. It was Colorado's coal capital for eighty years and a farm town of a few thousand for the forty after that; then it grew 66 percent between 2010 and 2020 and kept going. Most of the town is subdivisions built since 2000, in every direction, with a compact historic downtown on Briggs Street that has filled up with restaurants and breweries in the last decade. Fifty-eight percent of residents live on the Weld County side, which includes downtown and Town Hall; the rest are in Boulder County.

## Housing

Mostly single-family homes in master-planned neighborhoods: Vista Ridge, Erie Highlands, Flatiron Meadows, Colliers Hill, Erie Commons, Morgan Hill, Parkdale and others, most with metropolitan districts and homeowners' associations whose fees and mill levies vary a lot. The old grid around Briggs Street has the small houses from the mining era. The 2020 census counted 10,047 households with a median household income of $99,804. The listings sites in the sidebar show what is for sale; ask which county, which school district and which metro district before you fall for a house, because all three change from one street to the next.

## Schools

Most of Erie is in [St. Vrain Valley Schools](https://www.svvsd.org/), based in Longmont: Erie, Red Hawk and Black Rock elementaries, Soaring Heights PK-8, Erie Middle School and Erie High School (the Tigers), plus Aspen Ridge Preparatory charter. The Boulder County side is in the [Boulder Valley School District](https://www.bvsd.org/), which runs Meadowlark PK-8 in Erie and feeds Angevine Middle and Centaurus High in Lafayette. The Town's [schools page](https://www.erieco.gov/302/Schools) lists them all, with the private options.

## Getting around

By car. Arapahoe Road and Colorado 7 (Baseline Road) run west to Boulder in about twenty minutes; Erie Parkway and Colorado 52 reach I-25 in ten, and from there Denver is about thirty-five minutes and Denver International Airport about the same by E-470. US 287 runs along the west edge toward Longmont and Broomfield. There is little transit; nearly every trip is by car or bike. The town's own [trail network](/places/coal-creek-trail/) along Coal Creek connects the neighborhoods to downtown and the Community Center off-road.

## Utilities, trash, fire, police

- **Water and sewer** are Town of Erie utilities; start service through [Utility Billing](https://www.erieco.gov/581/Utility-Billing) at Town Hall, 303-926-2700.
- **Electricity** is Xcel Energy or United Power, depending on the address.
- **Natural gas** is Xcel Energy or Black Hills Energy, again by address.
- **Trash, recycling and compost** are private. The Town does not run collection; pick from its [list of licensed haulers](https://www.erieco.gov/1284/Utility-Providers) (Republic, Waste Connections, Waste Management, Western Disposal, One Way, Wompost for compost), and check your HOA first, since many neighborhoods contract one hauler.
- **Police** is the [Erie Police Department](https://www.erieco.gov/246/Police-Department) at 1000 Telleen Avenue; non-emergency 303-441-4444.
- **Fire and emergency medical** response is [Mountain View Fire Rescue](https://www.mvfpd.org/), a separate district that also serves Niwot, Mead and Dacono.
- **Building permits, planning and zoning** are Town departments; the [Planning Commission](/events/) meets on the first and third Wednesdays.

## Government

Erie is a home-rule town with a Town Council of a mayor and six members and an appointed Town Manager. Council meets on the [second and fourth Tuesdays](/events/) at 6:30 pm at Town Hall in the old Lincoln School, streamed live. The Town Manager position is being filled in autumn 2026, with a public meet-and-greet of the finalists on September 28. Boards for planning, parks and trails, historic preservation, sustainability, trees and the airport are filled by application.

## Living here

Summer is the Thursday farmers market on Briggs Street from May to mid-September, the splash pads at Coal Creek and Lehigh parks, the lazy river at the [Community Center](/places/erie-community-center/), Town Fair and the fireworks. Fall is Tails & Ales at the dog park, Brewfest and Boo! on Briggs Street downtown, and the corn maze at [Anderson Farms](/places/anderson-farms/). Winter is the Parade of Lights on the first Friday of December, the rink at Coal Creek Park, and the sledding hill at Community Park. Year-round there is trivia and music at the [Old Mine](/places/the-old-mine/), the [singletrack](/places/erie-singletrack/) on the north side, and a walk around [Thomas Reservoir](/places/thomas-reservoir-loop/) at sunset.
