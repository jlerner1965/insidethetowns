The Colorado Connector (CoCo) train tax, Ballot Issue 7A, passed in unofficial returns from the November 3 election. If the counties certify that result by November 25, a 0.333 percent tax, 33 cents on a $100 purchase, starts in July 2027 in the 31 cities and towns of the Front Range Passenger Rail District, the district's financing plan says.

- **Will I pay it?** On purchases made inside the district. Fort Collins, Loveland, Longmont, Boulder, Louisville and Lafayette are in it. Niwot, Berthoud, Erie, Lyons, Johnstown, Windsor and the Carbon Valley towns are not.
- **Does the train stop here?** The first trains, in 2029, stop at eight stations between Denver Union Station and Fort Collins. They run through Niwot and Berthoud without stopping.
- **When does it start?** Tax collections in July 2027, according to the district's [financing plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/finance). Trains in 2029, paid for with money the State and RTD have already committed. Service south to Colorado Springs and Pueblo, which the tax pays for, is planned for mid-2032.

## Will I pay the tax?

Only on what you buy inside the district. A 2026 state law, [SB 26-172](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-172), cut the district down to the whole of 31 named municipalities plus the Sterling Ranch metropolitan districts in Douglas County, which the [district lists](https://www.frprdistrict.com/about-the-district):

Arvada, Boulder, Bow Mar, Broomfield, Centennial, Cherry Hills Village, Colorado Springs, Columbine Valley, Denver, Edgewater, Englewood, Federal Heights, Fort Collins, Glendale, Greenwood Village, Lafayette, Lakeside, Lakewood, Littleton, Longmont, Louisville, Loveland, Manitou Springs, Mountain View, Pueblo, Sheridan, Starkville, Superior, Trinidad, Westminster and Wheat Ridge.

A town that is not on the list collects nothing on sales there. Its residents pay the extra third of a cent when they shop in a district city, [KUNC reported](https://www.kunc.org/news/2026-11-04/tax-to-fund-front-range-passenger-rail-district-poised-to-pass): a Berthoud resident buying in Loveland, or a Niwot resident buying in Longmont or Boulder.

To check an address, use the district's [lookup](https://coloradoconnector.com/check/). The district says the lookup is not an official ruling on who could vote.

About 800,000 voters in Adams, Arapahoe, Douglas and Weld counties who live outside the district were sent ballots with 7A on them by mistake, [KUNC reported](https://www.kunc.org/politics/2026-10-08/800-000-colorado-voters-received-a-passenger-rail-ballot-question-they-cannot-vote-on). The state said only eligible voters' choices on 7A would be counted. Having 7A on a Weld County ballot did not mean you live in the district.

Local Return is money the district sends back to towns with a station for the area around it, set by population under [Resolution 2026-11](https://3f188e0d-5574-43ce-9858-6d55c6f4be7d.usrfiles.com/ugd/3f188e_4b0a551ac20c4d08bb0caff96aca94ee.pdf). Castle Rock and Palmer Lake are on the line south of Denver: the [delivery plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/delivery-plan) lists track work from Sedalia to Castle Rock and from Palmer Lake to Monument. Neither town is in the district, and neither has a station.

## Does the train stop in Niwot or Berthoud?

No. Both towns sit on the BNSF Railway tracks CoCo will use north of Denver, and neither is on the district's list of stations for any phase.

**Niwot.** Between Boulder and Longmont the trains run on BNSF's Front Range Subdivision ([delivery plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/delivery-plan)), the tracks beside the Diagonal Highway that cross Second Avenue and Niwot Road ([City of Boulder](https://bouldercolorado.gov/projects/railroad-quiet-zones)). From 2029 that means three trains each way a day through Niwot, with the nearest stops at Boulder Junction and downtown Longmont. Niwot is not one of the 31 municipalities, so sales in Niwot carry no CoCo tax.

**Berthoud.** The BNSF line runs through downtown Berthoud ([BizWest](https://bizwest.com/2023/12/11/proposed-route-selected-for-front-range-passenger-rail)), between the Longmont and Loveland stations. [KUNC reported](https://www.ksut.org/news/2024-03-11/first-train-carries-passengers-from-denver-to-longmont-along-proposed-front-range-rail-line) in 2024 that the train would pass through Berthoud without stopping. The town's mayor, Will Karspeck, raised concerns then about the noise and air pollution of trains through town. Berthoud is not in the district, so sales there carry no CoCo tax. The nearest station is Loveland's, on Railroad Avenue.

## When does it start?

- **The tax: July 2027.** The [financing plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/finance) says collections begin in July 2027, with 2028 the first full year. The [delivery plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/delivery-plan) says spring or summer 2027.
- **The trains: 2029.** Starter Service runs on RTD's tracks from Denver Union Station to Westminster, then on BNSF's to Fort Collins ([delivery plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/delivery-plan)). Existing State and RTD money pays for it, so it starts with or without the tax ([FAQ](https://coloradoconnector.com/faq/)). The state's April 2026 schedule had construction running from January 2027 to January 2029 ([CTIO](https://www.codot.gov/programs/ctio/assets/agenda-item-documents/2026-agenda-item-documents/april-2026/6b-board-presentation_joint-service-implementation-and-finances-final.pdf)).
- **Local Return: 2029.** The financing plan sets aside $51.3 million a year for 25 years, starting in 2029.

## When will CoCo reach Colorado Springs and Pueblo?

Mid-2032 is the district's target for trains south of Denver. The tax pays for what comes after 2029, according to the [delivery plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/delivery-plan) and the [financing plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/finance):

| Phase | Target | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| 1, Starter Service | 2029 | Three round trips a day, Denver to Fort Collins, eight stations. No new tax. |
| 2, Full Service | Mid-2032 | Four round trips a day Denver to Fort Collins. Two continue south, adding Littleton, Sterling Ranch, North Colorado Springs, Colorado Springs and Pueblo. A new Trinidad station for the Southwest Chief. |
| 3, Expanded Service | 2037 at the earliest | More trips |
| 4 and 5, Expanded Service | Mid-2045 at the earliest | Up to 10 round trips a day north of Denver and eight south of it |

The financing plan calls the dates for phases 3 to 5 the earliest possible, based on tax money as it comes in. It assumes $398 million in 30-year bonds issued in 2030 toward Phase 2. South of Denver the trains would run on the Consolidated Main Line and the Joint Line, owned by Union Pacific and BNSF together ([delivery plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/delivery-plan)).

**Five years or 20?** Both numbers come from the district, and they measure different things. Every station is to have service within five years of the new funding; adding trips up to the full schedule takes about 20 years ([delivery plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/delivery-plan)).

## Not settled yet

- **The final count.** Results stay unofficial until each county's canvass board certifies them, by November 25 ([El Paso County](https://clerkandrecorder.elpasoco.com/elections/election-information/), [Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/election-results)). Boulder County's canvass meeting is November 24 ([Boulder County](https://bouldercounty.gov/?p=149727)).
- **The BNSF agreement for 2029.** The State and BNSF have a term sheet; the binding access agreement is still to come ([delivery plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/delivery-plan)). The state's schedule aimed to finish it in summer 2026 ([CTIO](https://www.codot.gov/programs/ctio/assets/agenda-item-documents/2026-agenda-item-documents/april-2026/6b-board-presentation_joint-service-implementation-and-finances-final.pdf)). On November 6 the district's general manager, Sal Pace, said it was “close to wrapped up,” with talks continuing the next week ([Colorado Newsline](https://www.kunc.org/news/2026-11-10/colorado-connector-officials-look-to-finalize-railroad-agreements-after-voters-approve-tax-hike)).
- **Final design.** The same schedule put 90 percent design and updated cost estimates in November and December 2026, followed by the order to start construction.
- **Union Pacific.** South of Denver the trains need agreements with Union Pacific and BNSF, which own that line together. Those talks will likely begin in early 2027 ([Colorado Newsline](https://www.kunc.org/news/2026-11-10/colorado-connector-officials-look-to-finalize-railroad-agreements-after-voters-approve-tax-hike)). The district's plan marks every phase after Starter Service as pending those conversations.
- **Money for later phases.** Phase 2 rests on the tax from July 2027 and bonds in 2030; phases 3 to 5 depend on how fast tax money comes in ([financing plan](https://coloradoconnector.com/finance)).
- **Local Return terms.** Each station town's amount, uses and conditions go into an agreement with the district after the vote ([Resolution 2026-11](https://3f188e0d-5574-43ce-9858-6d55c6f4be7d.usrfiles.com/ugd/3f188e_4b0a551ac20c4d08bb0caff96aca94ee.pdf)).
