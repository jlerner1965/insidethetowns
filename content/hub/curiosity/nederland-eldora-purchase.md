As of November 13, 2026, Nederland has not bought Eldora. Powdr still owns and runs the ski area, and the Town says it is still pursuing a purchase.

- **Has it closed?** No. The $120 million purchase agreement the town board approved in January expired when its April 30 closing deadline passed. The Town’s authority to sell the bonds that would pay for it lapsed on July 19, and no bonds have been issued, according to a [draft resolution on the board’s November 9 agenda](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/133108).
- **Latest:** On November 9 the board took up that resolution, which would limit further spending on the deal, and discussed how to cover Eldora costs its 2026 budget did not include. [BusinessDen reports](https://businessden.com/nederland-faces-2m-deficit-as-eldora-acquisition-bills-pile-up/) the board put off the vote until its next meeting.
- **Unresolved:** a new agreement with Powdr, investors willing to buy the bonds, about $20 million in donations the Town is seeking, and how the Town would pay about $2.76 million in costs if the deal never closes.

## Why does Nederland want to buy Eldora?

Powdr, the Utah company that owns Eldora, announced in August 2024 that it was selling the resort, [Bond Buyer reported](https://www.bondbuyer.com/news/agreement-approved-by-colorado-town-to-buy-ski-resort). Nederland made its interest public that November, according to a [Town memo of November 19, 2024](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/116819).

The Town’s reasons are local control and money. In approving the purchase agreement, the board called Eldora “a strategic investment in community assets that generate broad public benefit and long-term financial value” ([Ordinance 882](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/127853)). It listed local stewardship, economic vitality, community development, keeping employees and reinvesting in the town.

The Town’s [July FAQ](https://www.nederlandco.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/17) adds a budget reason: owning the resort would help with Nederland’s recurring budget shortfalls through “a more stable revenue base.”

## How would the purchase be paid for?

Almost entirely with borrowed money. The Town would sell revenue bonds: loans from investors that are repaid only from the resort’s own income, such as lift tickets, rentals and food ([January 6 memo](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/126810)).

In January the board approved up to $225 million in bonds ([Ordinance 885](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/127855)). That is more than the price because the bonds were also to [improve the resort](https://www.bondbuyer.com/news/colorado-town-authorizes-bonds-to-buy-ski-resort), fund a reserve for loan payments and repay the Town’s own deal costs, [Bond Buyer reported](https://www.bondbuyer.com/news/agreement-approved-by-colorado-town-to-buy-ski-resort).

The Town offered the bonds to investors in the spring; its offering papers are dated March 19 and May 13 ([public-records page](https://www.nederlandco.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/27)). The ordinance gave it 180 days to sell them. That window closed on July 19 with no bonds issued.

In its [September 30 statement](https://www.nederlandco.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/21), the Town said prospective bondholders “told us there were a few things they would like to see.” Its answer, set out there and in an [October 20 overview](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/132791), has two parts:

- **A sale-leaseback.** At closing, Powdr would transfer the resort to the Town’s Mountain Recreation Enterprise. The enterprise would hand legal title to the land and buildings to a trustee acting for the bondholders, and the trustee would lease the resort straight back to the enterprise to run. If the bonds went unpaid, bondholders could have the trustee bring in another operator without going through foreclosure. Title returns to the enterprise once the bonds are repaid.
- **About $20 million in donations** to pay for planned improvements, so the Town can borrow less, as the [Colorado Sun reported](https://coloradosun.com/2026/10/08/nederland-eldora-ski-area-purchase-donations-bonds/). The State of Colorado is the first contributor the Town says it is exploring.

Borrowing has also become more expensive. The Town says it first modeled interest at about 7.5 percent and now models closer to 8.5 percent.

## What has the Town already spent?

As of its October 20 list of bills paid, the Town had spent $1,195,949 on the purchase, all charged to its General Fund, the account that pays for everyday town operations ([draft resolution](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/133108)). The same draft says the 2026 budget did not include money for these costs. The Town lists the payments on its [public-records page](https://www.nederlandco.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/27).

If the deal fails, the Town’s estimate of the costs it would carry has grown:

- about $2.5 million in its [September 30 statement](https://www.nederlandco.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/21);
- about $2.76 million in the draft resolution on the November 9 agenda, which cites the Town’s updated September 30 estimates.

Both are more than the Town has paid so far, because they include costs run up but not yet paid. The two Town figures carry the same date, and the records we read do not explain the difference. News reports round the newer one to $2.8 million ([Colorado Sun](https://coloradosun.com/2026/11/11/eldora-mountain-acquisition-investigation/), [BusinessDen](https://businessden.com/nederland-faces-2m-deficit-as-eldora-acquisition-bills-pile-up/)).

## What happens if the deal falls through?

The Town would carry its costs itself. That can seem to contradict the Town’s assurance that residents won’t pay for Eldora, but the assurance covers a different bill. There are four parts:

1. **Repaying the bonds.** If the deal closes, the bonds would be repaid from the resort’s income alone. “Residents are not responsible for repaying the bonds, and the Town’s general fund is not pledged,” the Town wrote in its [January 6 memo](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/126810) to the board. That covers the loan, nothing else.
2. **Costs before closing.** The fees of the outside firms working on the deal are paid now, by the Town, from its General Fund ([draft resolution](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/133108)). Some contracts “will need to be paid regardless of closing,” then-Town Manager Jonathan Cain told [BusinessDen](https://businessden.com/nederlands-eldora-bills-top-800k-with-purchase-still-yet-to-close/) in July.
3. **Recovering them if it closes.** The Town would be repaid from the bond proceeds ([public-records page](https://www.nederlandco.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/27)).
4. **Carrying them if it fails.** Nothing repays them. The September 30 statement said the Town would pay them from its CSAFE investments, savings the board began investing in 2023.

The January memo also says the Town “is not obligated to close the transaction unless bond financing is successfully completed, ensuring that no liability is incurred absent full funding.” That protects the Town from having to buy Eldora without the money. By the Town’s own estimate, it does not protect the roughly $2.76 million spent on trying.

An earlier version of the Town’s [FAQ](https://www.nederlandco.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/22) said, “No general Town funds or taxpayer dollars are being used to purchase Eldora Mountain Resort.” The purchase price would come from the bonds. The costs of pursuing it have been charged to the General Fund.

## What could this mean for residents?

For now, the effect is on the Town’s budget. The General Fund took in $3.2 million in 2025 and is forecast to bring in $3 million in 2026, and the Eldora costs leave a shortfall of about $2 million, [BusinessDen reports](https://businessden.com/nederland-faces-2m-deficit-as-eldora-acquisition-bills-pile-up/). The [Colorado Sun](https://coloradosun.com/2026/11/11/eldora-mountain-acquisition-investigation/), citing an analysis by former trustee Dallas Masters, puts the 2026 deficit at $2.3 million.

On November 9, under the 2027 budget item ([packet](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/133108)), the board was shown ways to cover it. The Colorado Sun lists three:

- dissolving the Nederland Downtown Development Authority and using its $1.9 million in reserves;
- moving $2 million from water and sewer savings, with no expectation of repayment;
- moving the $900,000 the Town set aside for its community center.

[BizWest](https://bizwest.com/2026/11/11/nederland-digging-budget-hole-with-quest-for-eldora/) adds paid parking to the ideas under consideration. Interim Town Manager Miranda Gordon told the Sun the Town has the money in those funds “to be able to pursue one or more of the options.” The options were discussed, not voted on.

If the deal closes, the January memo says the bonds are not paid back with property or sales taxes and the plan creates no new taxes. The July FAQ says the Town “does not currently anticipate seeking a voter-approved tax increase” to pay the deal’s expenses.

Under the sale-leaseback, a trustee would hold title to the resort’s land and buildings for the life of the bonds. The Town’s documents do not say how the new structure changes the risk to the Town and its residents, [BizWest notes](https://bizwest.com/2026/11/11/nederland-digging-budget-hole-with-quest-for-eldora/).

## What has been confirmed for skiers?

The sale has not closed, so Powdr still owns and runs Eldora. On November 13, [Eldora’s website](https://www.eldora.com/) was selling [2026-27 season passes](https://www.eldora.com/tickets-passes/passes/), listed the resort as an Ikon Pass partner and said the season was “less than 14 days away.” Neither the home page nor its [hours page](https://www.eldora.com/the-mountain/questions/hours-faq/) gave an opening date.

The expired January agreement shows what the Town had planned for after a closing. Powdr would keep running the mountain for two years under a transition agreement, and closing depended on a new Forest Service permit and an agreement with Alterra Mountain Company, which runs the Ikon Pass ([January 6 memo](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/126810)).

The Town now expects to “negotiate updated agreements” if its financing comes together, and is working toward “a closing schedule that meets Eldora’s operational needs” ([September 30 statement](https://www.nederlandco.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/21)). “The middle of a ski season is realistically not the time that things are going to happen,” Trustee Andrew Dewart said on November 9, [BusinessDen reports](https://businessden.com/nederland-faces-2m-deficit-as-eldora-acquisition-bills-pile-up/).

## What happens next?

- **November 17, 7 p.m.:** the next regular board meeting, at the Nederland Community Center ([Town schedule](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/Portal/MeetingSchedule.aspx)). Its agenda was not yet posted on November 13. On October 20 the Town committed to an Eldora update at every board meeting ([update](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/132791)).
- **The spending resolution.** The version on the November 9 agenda was revised by the Town Attorney from a proposal by Dallas Masters, a resident in the Town’s records and a former trustee in news reports. It would limit new deal work to what is needed for a term sheet (a summary of proposed terms), capped at $150,000 without a further board vote, require board approval for any new consultant and require a written cost report each month ([packet](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/133108)).
- **Masters’ amendments.** In comments filed in the same packet, he asked for deadlines: a term sheet by January 31, 2027, a signed purchase agreement by March 31 and a closing by June 30, with the pursuit ending if a date is missed and not extended. He also asked that spending stop once total costs pass $3 million unless the board votes to continue.

Still open on November 13:

- whether Powdr will sign a new agreement, and at what price;
- whether investors will buy bonds under the new structure, which would also need a new bond ordinance ([draft resolution](https://nederlandco.civicweb.net/document/133108));
- whether the State or other donors will contribute;
- how the Town will cover its costs in the 2027 budget;
- what the board recorded on November 9, since the minutes were not yet posted.
