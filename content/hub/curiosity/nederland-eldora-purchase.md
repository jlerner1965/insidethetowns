Nederland, a town of about 1,500 people, has spent nearly two years trying to buy Eldora, the 680-acre Boulder County ski area, from its operator, Powdr. In January the town board approved a $120 million purchase and up to $225 million in bonds, to be repaid from the ski area's own revenue. The deal was meant to close by April 30. In September the town said it was having trouble selling the bonds and would seek $20 million in donations and help from the state, as the Colorado Sun and BusinessDen report.

## The board meeting of November 9

The town has spent about $1.2 million on the purchase in 2025 and 2026, and would carry about $2.8 million in costs if the deal falls through. At the November 9 meeting the board looked at ways to cover a shortfall of about $2 million in its general fund, BusinessDen reports. The options the Colorado Sun lists:

- dissolving the Nederland Downtown Development Authority and using its $1.9 million in reserves;
- moving $2 million from the water and sewer funds' savings;
- moving the $900,000 set aside for the community center.

The board also took up a resolution from a former trustee, Dallas Masters, that would have set deadlines to close the deal or stop spending, and a $3 million limit on spending. The board took out the deadlines and the limit. The version it discussed would limit further spending on the purchase, have the board approve any new consultants and have the town manager report on the deal. The final vote is expected at a later meeting.

## What comes next

If the deal closes, the costs are to be repaid from the sale of the bonds. One trustee, Andrew Dewart, said the deal is unlikely to close in the middle of the ski season, BusinessDen reports.
