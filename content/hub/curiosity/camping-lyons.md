Lyons sits where the two St. Vrain canyons open onto the plains, and the camping follows that shape. There is one campground in town, on the river, run by the Town. Everything else is up Colorado 7 or the Peak to Peak Highway, between 8,350 and 9,500 feet, run by the Forest Service or the National Park Service, and shut by snow for half the year.

The entries below run from town upward. Every fee, date and count is from the manager's own page or the federal booking site, and each says where.

## Before you pick one

The four Forest Service campgrounds have no hookups, no showers and no dump station. They take reservations through recreation.gov on a rolling six-month window, so a site for a July Saturday is released in January.

The 2027 dates below are the ones recreation.gov had posted on November 13, 2026. Snow can move an opening, and the Forest Service keeps the [current status of each campground](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/arp/conditions) in one table. Check [fire restrictions](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/arp/alerts/fire-restrictions) the week you go.

## Where you cannot camp

Several places near Lyons that look like they might allow it do not.

- [Bohn Park](https://www.townoflyons.com/350/Bohn-Park): the Town allows no camping of any kind, tent or RV.
- [Hall Ranch](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/parks-and-trails/hall-ranch/) and [Heil Valley Ranch](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/parks-and-trails/heil-valley-ranch/): Boulder County open space, day use only, no camping.
- [Button Rock Preserve](https://longmontcolorado.gov/facilities/button-rock-preserve/): the City of Longmont allows no camping and no overnight parking.
- The South St. Vrain canyon along Colorado 7: a [Forest Service order](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r02/arp/publication/alerts/Exhibit3Boulder.pdf) closes national forest land within a quarter mile of the highway from sundown to sunrise, outside designated campgrounds and sites.
