Go looking for ghost towns around Nederland and you find less than the name promises, and it is better to know that before the drive. Caribou, the mining town up the road, burned twice and emptied; the [Forest Service's history of the district](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/arp/recreation/discover-history) says "little remains." What is left in these hills is scattered: a mine camp preserved on county open space, a railroad grade, an assay office that is now a museum.

So this list is ordered by how much there is to see, not by fame. Each entry says who owns the ground, because around here the answer is often "partly private," and the agencies say so plainly.

## Two rules that apply everywhere here

Old mine workings are not safe and mostly not public. Where an agency says stay on the road or do not trespass, the entry repeats it.

Roads here close by date and by snow. The Forest Service keeps the [current status of its roads](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/arp/conditions) in one table, and its free [Motor Vehicle Use Map](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/arp/maps-guides/motor-vehicle-use-maps-mvum) shows which roads are open to which vehicles.

## Eldora is not one

Eldora, up the valley west of Nederland, is an old mining town with a [historic district on the National Register](https://www.historycolorado.org/location/eldora-historic-district), listed in 1989. People live there. The Forest Service describes it today as a cluster of small, mostly seasonal cabins. Drive through it; do not treat it as a ruin.
