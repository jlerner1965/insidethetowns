Colorado wolf 2516 traveled about 9,000 miles after being brought from British Columbia to Colorado in January 2025. Her movements took her across Colorado and into neighboring states before her tracking collar sent a mortality alert from Utah on November 7, 2026, [Colorado Parks and Wildlife (CPW) said](https://cpw.state.co.us/news/11132026/media-statement-confirmed-mortality-wolf-2516-utah).

CPW announced her death on November 13. The cause remains unknown, and the agency has not disclosed where in Utah she died. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service is investigating with Colorado and Utah wildlife and law enforcement officials.

## Circling western Colorado

Released in Eagle County, 2516 repeatedly crossed western Colorado rather than settling into one area.

At a July 2025 wildlife commission meeting, Eric Odell, then CPW's wolf program manager, traced her route: west from Eagle County toward Grand Junction, back up toward Steamboat Springs, down through Middle Park and South Park, and through the Gunnison and Montrose areas back toward Grand Junction. [KDNK's reporting](https://www.kdnk.org/local-news/2025-08-01/new-grey-wolf-pups-packs-and-predation) identified the traveling female as 2516. She "really has not stopped moving," Odell said.

The [Steamboat Pilot's account](https://www.steamboatpilot.com/news/colorado-wolf-activity-map-july-2025) of the same meeting put her distance at 2,469 miles between January 16 and June 30, 2025, about 15 miles a day during that period. That was an early snapshot of her travels, not an average across her whole journey. By its November 2026 announcement, CPW put her total at about 9,000 miles.

## The San Luis Valley, January 2026

On January 2 and 3, 2026, she was in the San Luis Valley, mostly on the Valley floor, [the Alamosa Citizen reported](https://www.alamosacitizen.com/wolf-confirmed-on-the-valley-floor). Other wolves had entered the Valley's watershed in 2025; she was the first to cross its lower elevations.

That week a CPW wildlife manager called Loren Buss, who owns land near Monte Vista, to say a wolf was in the area. Buss had no livestock there and passed the warning to the livestock owner who uses the land. Two days later, her collar was pinging north of the Valley.

Rick Basagoita, a CPW area manager, told the paper the agency tries to notify landowners and livestock producers near a wolf's route.

## What the maps can tell us

CPW's monthly wolf activity maps show watersheds where GPS collars placed wolves. A highlighted watershed means at least one collared wolf was in it during the month, [CPW says](https://cpw.state.co.us/wolf-sightings); it does not mean wolves traveled throughout the watershed or occupy it now.

The maps show the broad reach of Colorado's collared wolves. They do not show an individual wolf's route, do not capture wolves without collars, and do not establish that a wolf passed through a particular town. The route above is from Odell's briefing, not from the public maps.

## Where Colorado's wolf program stands

CPW reported 45 known wolves in Colorado as of September 24, 2026, including 20 pups born that year, [KRDO reported](https://krdo.com/news/2026/09/30/cpw-pauses-wolf-depredation-directive/). That is a dated count, not a live total after 2516's death.

Adult survival in the last biological year, April 1, 2025, through March 31, 2026, was 61 percent. CPW compared that with about 75 percent for an unharvested wolf population, [Colorado Politics reported](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/10/01/cpw-halts-removal-of-problem-wolves-sparking-rural-outcry/).

On September 30 the agency temporarily suspended its chronic depredation directive, its guidance for killing wolves that repeatedly attack livestock, and said it would weigh future removals case by case "using a very conservative approach," Colorado Politics reported. On October 1 a CPW spokesman said the agency had not identified a source of wolves for this winter and was still exploring options, [Cowboy State Daily reported](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2026/10/01/colorado-backs-off-killing-wolves-that-attack-cattle-as-population-stalls-at-45/).

## Not settled yet

CPW has confirmed that 2516 died in Utah. What caused her death remains unresolved.

The November 13 announcement did not disclose a precise location or findings explaining the death. Until investigators release more information, her recorded movements show how far she traveled, but not what ended her journey.
