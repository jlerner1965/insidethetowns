Fort Lupton sits on US 85 and the South Platte, eight miles east of the Carbon Valley towns and 35 to 40 minutes from Denver. Downtown is Denver Avenue, which the City calls its historic main street, and the museum is a few blocks away on First Street. Leave the car on or near Denver Avenue; everything below is on it or close to it. The one thing that is not, the rebuilt fort northwest of town, is a drive and is open two weekends a month, so it goes at the end.

## 01 · Denver Avenue

The City points to the early-20th-century commercial buildings along [Denver Avenue](/places/historic-denver-avenue/) and keeps a register of eleven designated historic sites; on this street it lists the Odd Fellows Lodge at 300–306 Denver Avenue and the Seymour and Birdie Rhode House at 149. The 300 block has three doors worth knowing: [Lucky Shot](/places/lucky-shot-sports-bar-and-grill/) at 327, a sports bar and billiards hall with six pool tables that opens at 11 am on weekdays and 10:30 am at weekends; [A Natural Way](/places/a-natural-way/) at 333, a wellness shop with vitamins, herbs and its own mushroom coffee; and its cafe, [Raw Roots](/places/raw-roots-juice-bar-and-cafe/) at 337, for juice, smoothies, coffee, paninis and soup, Tuesday to Thursday 9 am–6 pm, Friday 9 am–2 pm, Saturday 9 am–1 pm, closed Sunday and Monday.

*Getting there:* park on or near the 300 block. *Best for:* the building fronts, a coffee or a juice first thing, a game of pool later.

## 02 · Wholly Stromboli

[Wholly Stromboli](/places/wholly-stromboli/) at 410 Denver Avenue fills the St. John building, the mercantile Edgar St. John put up in 1889 and rebuilt as a single storey after a fire; the City's historic tour places it at the north-east corner of Denver Avenue and Fourth Street. The dining room keeps its seventeen-foot stamped-tin ceilings, the kitchen makes its dough, marinara, meatballs and strombolis by hand, and there is a bar, a veranda and a speakeasy room for events. It takes no reservations, seats a party once everyone has arrived and runs an online waitlist. Hours are Tuesday to Thursday 11 am–9 pm, Friday 11 am–9:30 pm, Saturday noon–9:30 pm and Sunday noon–8:30 pm; it is closed Monday. On a Monday, [Santiago's](/places/santiagos/) at 150 First Street opens at 6 am Monday to Saturday, and Lucky Shot is open every day.

*Getting there:* one block north along Denver Avenue. *Best for:* the meal the visit is built around.

## 03 · The City Museum

The [Fort Lupton City Museum](/places/fort-lupton-city-museum/) at 453 First Street is the town's 1929 public library, designed by John J. Huddart, one of his last works before he died in 1930; the collection moved in during 1993 and the building was designated historic in 1996. It began with the arrowhead collection Ralph Haynes, one of the first mail carriers, gave the City in the 1950s, and now holds the story of the canning factory, a 1950s domestic corner, yearbooks and newspapers, and an archive of family histories, cemetery records and photographs back to the 1800s. Admission is free. It opens Wednesday to Friday, 10 am–3 pm, and on no other day, so a weekday visit is the one that includes it.

*Getting there:* a few blocks from the Denver Avenue storefronts, on First Street. *Best for:* an hour of local history, free.

## 04 · House of Smoke

Back on Denver Avenue, further north at 825, [House of Smoke](/places/house-of-smoke/) has smoked meats since 1973, when Gordon Barsness and his family bought their first smoker; the business still works from its original building, now tripled in size. It lists more than three hundred items, from smoked turkeys to thirty kinds of wild-game sausage and jerky, and its deli sells sandwiches and meat trays, with a wild boar and Hatch chile burger on Wednesdays and a bison or elk burger on Fridays. It is open weekdays only, 8 am–3 pm.

*Getting there:* a short walk or drive north along Denver Avenue. *Best for:* a weekday lunch, and sausage and jerky to take home.

## The fort, on the right weekend

The South Platte Valley Historical Society's historic park at 2001 Historic Park Way, northwest of town off US 85, has the rebuilt 1830s trading fort the city is named for. It opens to walk-in visitors on the first and third weekends of each month from May to October, 10 am–4 pm each Saturday and Sunday, with a suggested donation of $5 for anyone 13 and over; in 2026 the last of those weekends is October 17 and 18, and its free Pumpkinfest with the City is on October 10, 3–8 pm. It does not have a listing on this guide yet; [the Society's site](https://www.spvhs.org/) has the current dates.

## A simple first visit

1. Park on or near the 300 block of Denver Avenue.
2. Walk the storefronts, with a coffee or a juice at Raw Roots (not Sunday or Monday).
3. Lunch at Wholly Stromboli (not Monday), or a weekday deli lunch at House of Smoke.
4. The City Museum, if it is Wednesday, Thursday or Friday.
5. On a first or third weekend from May to October, drive out to the fort.

## Getting here

Drive times are estimates for normal conditions and vary with traffic, weather and construction; check a live routing service before you set out. Fort Lupton is about 35 to 40 minutes from Denver on US 85, and the Carbon Valley towns are eight miles west; the Historical Society's directions put its park ten miles east of I-25 on Highway 52 and nine miles west of I-76. No official page we read lists a bus service to downtown, so none is claimed. Posted signs override anything written here.

## Accessibility: what we can confirm

None of the official pages read for this walk says anything about step-free entrances, accessible parking or public restrooms at the stops above: the museum's page gives its hours and free admission and no more, and the restaurants and shops publish none of it. So nothing is confirmed and nothing is claimed here. Contact a business directly before relying on it.
