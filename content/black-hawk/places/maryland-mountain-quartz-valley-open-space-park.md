Maryland Mountain Quartz Valley Open Space Park is the City of Black Hawk's day-use park for walking, hiking, running, mountain biking, picnicking and wildlife watching. The City opened the Hidden Treasure Trailhead, at 8,205 feet north of downtown, in 2020, with a bike and pedestrian bridge over Highway 119 to the Historic Gilpin Tramway trail and the rest of the system. The trails wind around Maryland Mountain, Chase Gulch, Quartz Valley and the old narrow-gauge Gilpin Tramway, past the Bonanza Mill, the Belden & Tennal Stamp Mill, the Robert Emmet Mine and the Queen of the West Mine; the City names the Historic Gilpin Tramway, Easy Money, Summit, Paymaster, Claim Jumper and Quartz Valley trails, and the summit at 9,200 feet. A second trailhead at Gregory and High streets, Bates Hill Park, is described as coming soon.

The park is open dawn to dusk. Dogs must be leashed (a leash no longer than 10 feet, under the City code), and there are dog-waste stations at the trailhead and on the tramway trail. Restrooms and trash cans are at the trailhead only, and there is no water anywhere in the park. Hikers climbing have the right of way, and cyclists yield to hikers in both directions. Non-emergency concerns go to the Black Hawk Dispatch Center, 303-582-5878.

The park details were read on the City's Maryland Mountain page on 10 October 2026; the trail map it links is dated August 13, 2024. The City's news release of July 13, 2026 reported a tenth-of-an-acre wildfire in the park, put out by the Black Hawk Fire Department after two passers-by found it, and said Gilpin County and the City were then under fire restrictions banning open burning, fireworks and smoking near vegetation.
