Black Hawk's casinos are on Main Street and Richman Street; the City's own visitor projects are a pedestrian plaza on Gregory Street and an open space park on Maryland Mountain, reached by a bridge over Highway 119. The trailhead has its own lot, so this is a driving order: walk in the morning, then drive to the Monarch, leave the car in its garage and use the City's free tramway for Gregory Street.

## 01 · Maryland Mountain Quartz Valley Open Space Park

[Maryland Mountain](/places/maryland-mountain-quartz-valley-open-space-park/) is the City's day-use park for hiking, running and mountain biking, open dawn to dusk. Park at the Hidden Treasure Trailhead, 8060 Black Hawk Blvd, at 8,205 feet north of downtown, and cross the bike and pedestrian bridge over Highway 119 to the Historic Gilpin Tramway trail. From there the City's trails (Easy Money, Summit, Paymaster, Claim Jumper, Quartz Valley) pass old mills and mines on the way to a 9,200-foot summit. The City's map is dated August 2024; download it before you go, or scan the QR code at the trailhead. Dogs must be on a leash. Restrooms and trash cans are at the trailhead only, and there is no water at the trailhead or anywhere in the park, so bring your own.

*Getting there:* by car, to the trailhead lot at 8060 Black Hawk Blvd. *Best for:* a morning walk or ride, with the dog on a leash and water in the pack.

## 02 · Black Hawk & Central City Tramway

Drive back into town on Highway 119 to the [Monarch](/places/monarch-casino-resort-spa/) at 488 Main Street, whose page describes a nine-level covered self-parking garage, complimentary valet and EV charging. From there the [Black Hawk & Central City Tramway](/places/black-hawk-and-central-city-tramway/) is free. It runs from 10 am every day, every 30 minutes until 2 am Monday to Thursday, and every 20 to 30 minutes until 3 am Friday to Sunday. The City's route brochure numbers 19 stops, with Monarch Casino at stop 2 and Gregory Plaza at stop 10; drivers stop only at designated stops. The buses are wheelchair accessible.

*Getting there:* on foot from the garage to the Monarch stop. *Best for:* getting to Gregory Street and back without moving the car.

## 03 · Gregory Street Plaza

[Gregory Street Plaza](/places/gregory-street-plaza/) is the City's pedestrian street in the HARD District, finished in 2021: brick paving, benches, history signs and a four-faced post clock inscribed with the founders' names, among restored houses moved and rebuilt for it. The City's brick fire house on the plaza was built to hold its 1935 Chevrolet fire truck. The plaza's shops and its pizzeria do not have listings on this guide yet. In October 2026 the City runs Halloween Street here, with 69 Halloween sculptures and a Pumpkin Passport all month, a free fall festival on Saturdays October 10, 17 and 24 from 11 am to 3 pm, and trick-or-treating on October 31.

*Getting there:* by tramway to the Gregory Plaza stop. *Best for:* an hour on foot around midday, and a Saturday in October for the festival.

## 04 · Spa Monarch

[Spa Monarch](/places/spa-monarch/) is back at the Monarch, open daily 8 am–9 pm: over 30,000 square feet with herbal steam rooms, a Laconium lounge and an indoor infinity pool on the 23rd floor (pool open 6 am–10 pm). A day pass gives four hours of facility use; passes are limited, must be reserved, and have blackout dates, so book one by phone (720-460-4000) before the day.

*Getting there:* by tramway back to the Monarch stop. *Best for:* the afternoon, after the trail.

## 05 · Bistro Mariposa or the Monarch Chophouse

Two dinners in the same building. [Bistro Mariposa](/places/bistro-mariposa/), on the third floor by the sportsbook, is contemporary Latin food with tequilas, mezcals and Colorado craft beer, open Wednesday, Thursday and Sunday 5–10 pm and Friday and Saturday 5–11 pm, closed Monday and Tuesday; its Wednesday social hour runs from 5 pm to close. The [Monarch Chophouse](/places/monarch-chophouse/), also on the third floor, is open every night, Sunday to Thursday 5–9 pm and Friday and Saturday 5–10 pm, with aged prime beef, seafood and Colorado lamb. Both take reservations.

*Getting there:* on foot inside the Monarch. *Best for:* dinner from 5 pm; the Chophouse on a Monday or Tuesday.

## A simple first visit

1. Water in the car, then the Hidden Treasure Trailhead for a morning on Maryland Mountain.
2. Drive to the Monarch's garage at 488 Main Street.
3. The free tramway to Gregory Plaza after 10 am, an hour on the plaza, and the tramway back.
4. A reserved afternoon at Spa Monarch.
5. Dinner at Bistro Mariposa from Wednesday to Sunday, or the Chophouse any night.

## Getting here

Drive times are estimates for normal conditions and vary with traffic, weather and construction; check a live routing service before you set out. Monarch's directions from Denver are I-70 west to the Central City Parkway exit (Exit 243), then the signs for Central City and Black Hawk onto Colorado 119 into town; it puts Denver International Airport about 55 miles away, one to one and a half hours by car. [COtrip](https://www.cotrip.org/) carries the live state of the highways.

Monarch's page also says there is no direct RTD route from Denver to Black Hawk, and points to an hourly shuttle service with pickups in Arvada, Aurora and Lakewood, whose schedules, locations and fees vary. Once in town, the tramway above is free.

## Accessibility: what we can confirm

The City says the tramway's buses are wheelchair accessible, and that a rider within three-quarters of a mile of the route can ask for an off-route pickup by calling MV Transportation dispatch at 303-761-3145, extension 1, at least two hours ahead. Monarch says each floor of its garage connects directly to the hotel tower.

The surfaces of the Maryland Mountain trails and the bridge, the plaza's paving and curb ramps, and step-free access inside the spa and the restaurants have not been confirmed and are not claimed here; contact the City or Monarch directly before relying on them.
