The Georgetown Energy Museum is in a working hydroelectric plant, owned and operated by Xcel Energy, that has generated power since 1900; the museum is a separate nonprofit that leases part of the plant. Its about page describes two Pelton water-wheel and generator sets of 720 kilowatts each, about 1.5 megawatts in all, fed through a 30-inch penstock from the Georgetown reservoir about 0.9 miles up the canyon toward Guanella Pass, with a 700-foot fall. The exhibits include photographs and household appliances from the early use of electricity. Admission is free, with donations welcome, and school groups can arrange visits by email.

The home page's banner says "Open 7 days a week 11–5, Memorial Day thru Labor Day", and the text lower down says "open daily from Memorial Day through the first week in October"; either way it is closed now. Address, phone and hours were read on the museum's home and contact pages on 10 October 2026. The Town of Georgetown's History and Tours page (https://georgetown-colorado.org/history-and-tours/, last updated 16 July 2025) also lists it at 600 Griffith Street.
