The Manitou Cliff Dwellings are a preserve of relocated stones, not an original settlement: in the early 1900s Virginia McClurg and Harold Ashenhurst's Colorado Cliff Dwellers Association collected stones from some twenty-five mesa-top dwellings on farmland near Dolores, Colorado, shipped them by rail to Colorado Springs and rebuilt several structures in a canyon in Manitou Springs renamed Cliff Canyon; the preserve opened in 1907. Visitors can walk around and through the rooms (but not into the kiva). A three-story pueblo building in the Taos style, built around the turn of the century, was later expanded into museums of pottery and artifacts and a gift shop. Leashed pets may go everywhere, including the ruins and the museums. The dwellings are not wheelchair or scooter accessible; a ramp from the parking lot gives a view of the whole front. Parking is free, with limited room for buses, RVs and trailers in a lower lot about 200 yards away.

The site is open year-round, weather permitting: 9 am to 5 pm from April through October, 9 am to 4:30 pm in March, 9 am to 4 pm in November and 10 am to 4 pm from December to February, and the park stays open 30 minutes after the last admission. It closes on Thanksgiving and Christmas Day. Admission is $12.75 plus tax for adults 12 and over, $8 for children 4 to 11, $10 for seniors 60 and over and free for children 3 and under; groups of ten or more pay less.
