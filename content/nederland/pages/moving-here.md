## Who lives here

Nederland is a statutory town in Boulder County, governed under the Colorado Revised Statutes by a Board of Trustees of a mayor and six trustees. Trustees are elected at large for four-year terms and the mayor for two, the Board meets on the first and third Tuesdays, and its directives are carried out by Town staff led by the Town Administrator ([Board of Trustees](https://nederlandco.org/1231/Board-of-Trustees)). Town Hall is at 45 West First Street, open Monday to Friday from 9 to 5, 303-258-3266 ([Town Hall](https://nederlandco.org/1194/Town-Hall)).

The 2020 Census counted 1,471 people ([2020 Census, in the State Demography Office's census file](https://storage.googleapis.com/co-publicdata/historical-census.csv)); the office's 2024 estimate is 1,417 ([DOLA population series](https://gis.dola.colorado.gov/lookups/countymuni?placefips=53175&year=2000,2010,2020,2021,2022,2023,2024&compressed=no); the same figures are in the office's [municipal lookup](https://demography.dola.colorado.gov/assets/lookups/muni_pop_lookup.html)). The Town's own [history page](https://nederlandco.org/1444/History) says the name was chosen in 1874, "when the town was incorporated", that the tungsten boom took the population to nearly 3,000 by 1916, that it fell to about 200 by 1920, and that growth in the 1990s came from "new residents who commuted all along the Front Range for work" before leveling out at the turn of this century.

The Town has no police department of its own: it contracts with the Boulder County Sheriff's Office, whose Patrol Section covers Nederland, Lyons and Superior 24 hours a day ([Sheriff's Office](https://bouldercounty.gov/safety/sheriff/law/operations/)). The non-emergency number is 303-441-4444 ([Town law-enforcement page](https://nederlandco.org/1451/Law-Enforcement)).

## Housing

The State Demography Office counts 778 housing units in the town in 2024, 685 of them occupied and 93 vacant, up from 749 units in 2010 ([DOLA housing series](https://gis.dola.colorado.gov/lookups/munipophousing?placefips=53175&year=2010,2020,2024&stats=totalpopulation,householdpopulation,totalhousingunits,occupiedhousingunits,vacanthousingunits&compressed=no)). Neither the Town nor the county publishes a dated sale-price figure for Nederland on the pages we read, so this guide gives none; the listings in the sidebar show what is for sale.

Through 2023 the Town ran a [Housing Needs Assessment](https://nederlandco.org/1381/Housing-Needs-Assessment), and in December 2023 the Board adopted five ordinances changing Chapter 16 of the Municipal Code "to support an increase in affordable and attainable housing development", covering district categories, use groups, yard and bulk, accessory dwelling units and a residential development administrative variance; they were codified in January 2024 ([final documentation](https://nederlandco.org/1382/Final-Housing-Needs-Assessment-Documenta)). What a lot may be used for depends on its zone in Chapter 16, shown on the Town's [zoning map](https://nederlandco.org/1304/Zoning). The Town's current Comprehensive Plan is dated 2025, and it has a land-use intergovernmental agreement with Boulder County "that identifies areas appropriate for development and those prioritized for preservation" ([Community Master Plans](https://nederlandco.org/1374/Community-Master-Plans)).

Two building rules that matter when you buy or build: since the Town adopted the 2018 building codes, defensible space is required on all new or remodeled buildings, residential and commercial, with a mitigation plan reviewed by the fire district ([Wildfire Mitigation Plan](https://nederlandco.org/1301/Wildfire-Mitigation-Plan)); and Chapter 11 of the code requires a culvert in every driveway that meets a public street ([Streets](https://nederlandco.org/1323/Streets)). Outside the town limits, in unincorporated Boulder County, a building permit for a new home, an addition over 200 square feet or deck work carries wildfire-mitigation requirements, with extra vegetation rules in west Boulder County, and a short-term or vacation rental in Wildfire Zone 1 needs a Wildfire Partners assessment before it is licensed ([Wildfire Partners](https://wildfirepartners.org/west-boulder-county/building-permits-short-term-rentals/)).

## Schools

Nederland is in the [Boulder Valley School District](https://www.bvsd.org/schools), which lists two schools in town: Nederland Elementary and Nederland Middle-Senior. [Nederland Elementary](https://nee.bvsd.org/) runs from preschool through fifth grade at 1 Sundown Trail, in a building from 1989 ([bond page](https://bond.bvsd.org/school-projects/nederland-elementary-school)). Nederland Middle-Senior is at 597 Eldora Road, built in 1971; the district's 2022 bond has a Phase 4 project there with a working budget of $10,589,000, including $2.86 million for career and technical education space ([bond page](https://bond.bvsd.org/school-projects/nederland-middlesr)). The district's [school finder](https://www.bvsd.org/departments/enrollment/k-12-school-finder) confirms the school for any address.

The district is shrinking. BVSD says it has lost enrollment since 2018, projects 1,700 fewer students in five years for a decline of more than 5,000 since 2017, and on 25 August 2026 presented the Board a "Resilient Schools" proposal of closures, consolidations and reconfigurations ([declining enrollment](https://www.bvsd.org/current-topics/declining-enrollment)); a March 2026 letter to the Nederland school communities said final decisions were anticipated for October ([bond page](https://bond.bvsd.org/school-projects/nederland-elementary-school)). The proposal pages we read on 6 October 2026 did not name either Nederland school; check them before you commit to a house for a school.

## Getting around

By car, on two state highways. The Town says CDOT owns and maintains Highway 119 from its junction with Highway 72 down to Boulder Falls, and Highway 72 from the traffic circle to Highway 7; most other roads in town are the Town's, and the same page lists Boulder County's Road Maintenance Division, District 3, at 303-441-3962 for grading, plowing, culverts and signs ([Streets](https://nederlandco.org/1323/Streets)). CDOT's $31 million repair of the 15-mile stretch of Colorado 119 between Boulder and Nederland, damaged in the 2013 flood, ran from 2019 to May 2021 and rebuilt the islands of the Nederland roundabout; its detours were Colorado 7, US 36 and Colorado 72 to the north, or Colorado 93 and Colorado 72 to the south ([CDOT](https://www.codot.gov/projects/archives/co-119-boulder-canyon-improvements)). Neither the Town's pages nor CDOT's give a drive time, so this guide does not either. Live conditions are on [COtrip](https://www.cotrip.org/).

Transit is RTD's NB bus. RTD's [schedule page](https://app.rtd-denver.com/en-US/route/NB/schedule) notes that service to Eldora runs only when the ski area is open. The Park-n-Ride is on West First Street; a $1.5 million DRCOG grant from 2019 is paying for a sidewalk from it to the Visitors Center, a fast EV charger and bike racks ([Transportation Improvement Project](https://nederlandco.org/1390/Transportation-Improvement-Project)). Full-time residents and employees in the Nederland Community Library District get an RTD EcoPass, paid for by property taxes, good for unlimited rides on RTD buses and trains including to the airport but not Access-a-Ride ([EcoPass](https://nederlandco.org/1468/EcoPass)). You collect it at Town Hall with photo ID and proof of residency ([how to get it](https://nederlandco.org/1472/How-to-Get-Your-EcoPass)); renters must show proof again every year, and the pass is valid on the N to Eldora ([FAQ](https://nederlandco.org/1470/FAQ)). The district is run by the Boulder County Commissioners, advised by a local committee, NEPPIDAC ([NEPPIDAC](https://nederlandco.org/1473/Nederland-Eco-Pass-Public-Improvement-Di)). The Town has written its first Multimodal Transportation Plan, dated 2024 ([plans](https://nederlandco.org/1374/Community-Master-Plans)).

## Services

**Water and sewer** are the Town's, from its own water plant and a wastewater plant built in 2013 ([Wastewater](https://nederlandco.org/1337/Wastewater)). The 2026 residential rates are quarterly: a water base fee of $49.20 in town or $73.80 outside it, usage at $10.24, $15.36 and $20.49 per 1,000 gallons in three tiers, a sewer base fee of $175.71 in town or $263.57 outside, and sewer usage at $11.09 per 1,000 gallons ([rates](https://nederlandco.org/1347/Residential-WaterSewer-Rates)). The water service line is the owner's to repair, not the Town's ([Public Works](https://nederlandco.org/1322/Public-Works)); meters are read remotely and flag leaks ([Water Meters](https://nederlandco.org/1352/Water-Meters)); the annual Water Quality Report is mailed to every account and posted ([reports](https://nederlandco.org/1329/Annual-Water-Reports)). Billing is 303-258-3266 ext. 4010, Tuesday to Friday ([Utilities](https://nederlandco.org/1328/Utilities)). The Town's pages name no trash hauler, electricity or gas provider, so ask the seller.

**Fire and ambulance** are the Nederland Fire Protection District, which the Town says it does not operate: it is a separate district paid by property taxes across a larger area, with full-time staff and volunteers ([Town](https://nederlandco.org/1450/Fire-Protection-District)). The district covers about 56 square miles including the Town, the Eldora townsite and the Eldora ski area, west to the Continental Divide ([service area](https://www.nfpd.org/service-area)); it answers about 500 calls a year, 65 to 70 percent of them medical ([about](https://www.nfpd.org/about-us)); and since 2020 its whole area is ISO class 4, where it had been 5 in town and 8b outside ([ISO](https://www.nfpd.org/iso-classification)). The district's address is 650 Lower West 4th Street; Station 1 holds its offices and most of its apparatus, and volunteer-staffed stations are at 2815 Ridge Road and 555 Eldorado Avenue in Eldora ([stations](https://www.nfpd.org/stations)). Non-emergency 303-258-9161 ([NFPD](https://www.nfpd.org/)).

**Police** is the Boulder County Sheriff, above. **Emergency alerts** come through Boulder County's Everbridge system, which the Town also uses for utility and road notices; sign up and add your addresses ([Everbridge](https://nederlandco.org/1313/Emergency-Alerts---Everbridge)). The Town's Office of Emergency Management is 720-643-9263 ([Emergency Management](https://nederlandco.org/1311/Emergency-Management)).

**Library** is the [Nederland Community Library](https://nederland.colibraries.org/location-and-hours/) at 200 Highway 72 North, open Monday to Thursday 10 to 7, Friday and Saturday 10 to 5, Sunday 1 to 5, 303-258-1101. Voters created the library district in November 2002, funded by a mill levy on property within five miles of Town Hall ([history](https://nederland.colibraries.org/ncl-a-short-history/)); its board meets on the fourth Wednesday ([board](https://nederland.colibraries.org/library-board-of-trustees/)).

**Parks and recreation** are the Town's Public Works, working to the plan of the Parks, Recreation and Open Space Advisory Board (PROSAB). The [parks list](https://nederlandco.org/1446/Trails-Open-Spaces) includes Chipeta Park at 254 Lakeview Drive, Barker Meadows Reservoir, Guercio Memorial Park's ball field, the Nathan Lazarus Skatepark at 155 East 1st Street, Mud Lake Open Space, and [NedRINK](https://nederlandco.org/1353/NedRINK) at 500 Indian Peaks Drive, open December to February, which the Town calls "Colorado's largest outdoor ice rink". The Community Center's [Fitness Place](https://nederlandco.org/1417/Fitness-Place) has a weight room, cardio deck, studio and gym, open Monday to Thursday 8 to 8, Friday 8 to 7 and Saturday 9 to 3.

**Eldora Mountain Resort** is inside the fire district (above). The resort is on the Ikon Pass and runs a Nordic center with 40 kilometers of trails ([Eldora](https://www.eldora.com/)). The Town has been trying for nearly two years to buy it from POWDR through a Mountain Recreation Enterprise Fund and revenue bonds, which it says uses no general funds or taxes and needs no public vote; on 30 September 2026 the Mayor and Board said they are "continuing to pursue the acquisition", are now modeling about 8.5 percent interest, and plan to raise about $20 million philanthropically for capital projects ([update](https://nederlandco.org/CivicAlerts.aspx?AID=21); [FAQ](https://nederlandco.org/CivicAlerts.aspx?AID=22)).

## Before you decide

- **Wildfire.** The Town says it "is no stranger to the threat of wildfire" ([Wildfire Resilience](https://nederlandco.org/1433/Wildfire-Resilience)). On 6 October 2026 the fire district had Stage 1 fire restrictions in effect for western Boulder County ([NFPD](https://www.nfpd.org/)). Boulder County's Wildfire Partners rebate program is open to anyone who owns or rents a home in the county ([rebates](https://wildfirepartners.org/rebate/)). Defensible space is required for any new or remodeled building in town (above).
- **The 2025 fire at 20 Lakeview Drive.** On 9 October 2025 a sheriff's deputy saw the Caribou Village Shopping Center on fire at 3:41 am; an evacuation order went out at 4:28 am for the south and west sides of Barker Reservoir and was lifted at 9:47 am; the two-story building on the south side was fully engulfed, and the Town asked residents to conserve water because so much had gone to firefighting ([Boulder County](https://bouldercounty.gov/news/structure-fire-at-caribou-village-shopping-center-in-nederland/)). The fire district's [Lakeview Fire page](https://www.nfpd.org/caribou-village-fire-20-lakeview-dr) says the cause investigation is the ATF's and, as of 27 January 2026, its reports were still unavailable.
- **Snow.** The Town quotes an average of 140 inches a year, with high winds and drifts, and says its plowing "is not on par with CDOT, Boulder County, or the City of Boulder" ([General Considerations](https://nederlandco.org/1326/General-Considerations)). Plows go out at 4 am only if there are 4 inches at 3 am on a weekday or 6 inches at 5 am on a weekend; cul-de-sacs are plowed last; parking on a street during a storm or with more than six inches of snow is prohibited, and the plow will not turn its blade for your driveway ([Snow Plowing](https://nederlandco.org/1325/Snow-Plowing); [FAQ](https://nederlandco.org/1327/Snow-Plowing-FAQ)).
- **Septic.** If the house is on a septic system rather than the Town's sewer: Boulder County has required since 2008 that a septic system be inspected and approved at sale, with a Property Transfer Certificate from Boulder County Public Health before closing (fee $500, up to ten business days) or a notarized agreement that the buyer repairs it within a year; a system under five years old is exempt ([SepticSmart](https://bouldercounty.gov/environment/water/septicsmart/property-transfer/)).
- **Which district.** The fire district says to check the property tax statement to see which fire district a house pays into ([service area](https://www.nfpd.org/service-area)); it holds a public hearing on 21 October 2026 on including Ridge Road ([hearing](https://www.nfpd.org/2026-10-21-public-hearings-ridge-road-inclusion-6pm)). The EcoPass follows the library district boundary, and a pass must be returned if you move out of it ([FAQ](https://nederlandco.org/1470/FAQ)).
- **The Town's water.** Out-of-town customers pay 50 percent more for water and sewer, the service line is yours to fix, and every account gets the annual water quality report (above).
- **Eldora.** The Town's FAQ says the purchase would not change property taxes or utility bills, and also that "there is a possibility that the acquisition and related financing may not close" ([FAQ 7.0](https://nederlandco.org/CivicAlerts.aspx?AID=17)).
