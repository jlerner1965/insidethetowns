[Hessie Trailhead](/places/hessie-trailhead/) is where Nederland's day hikers go for Lost Lake, Devils Thumb and the lakes under the Continental Divide. It is also, in the Forest Service's words, "extremely popular during the summer months", and its parking is limited. Boulder County, which provides access to the trailhead but does not manage the trails, runs a free shuttle for exactly that reason. Here is how the trailhead works, from the county and Forest Service pages as of October 2026.

## Getting there

The [Forest Service directions](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/arp/recreation/hessie-trailhead): from Nederland, go south on Colorado 119 for 0.6 mile, turn west on County Road 130 toward Eldora, and follow the paved road up the valley to the town of Eldora, where the pavement ends. Continue 0.75 mile on dirt to a fork; the left fork goes to Hessie. There are no restrooms at the trailhead.

## The parking problem

The Forest Service says parking is "limited and located near the trailhead and Hessie Cabin", and asks visitors to take the county shuttle on summer and fall weekends and holidays. Boulder County's [Hessie page](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/parks-and-trails/hessie-trailhead/) adds that the only roadside parking is in designated stretches along Fourth of July Road, between the signs only. Park outside them and the fine is $50 or a tow. The county calls the trailhead "extremely congested on weekends" and posts its own visitation counts by day, hour and month on the same page.

If you do want to drive, the county lists alternatives it would rather send you to: [Mud Lake](/places/mud-lake-open-space/) and Caribou Ranch (county open space, hiking only at Caribou), [Brainard Lake](/places/brainard-lake-recreation-area/) (Forest Service, reservation and day-use fee), the East Portal Trailhead at the Moffat Tunnel, the Sourdough Trail and Betasso Preserve.

## The Hessie Shuttle

The county's shuttle runs between Nederland and the trailhead on weekend days and holidays through the hiking season, free, with no ticket or reservation. 2026 season: Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays and holidays, May 22 through Sunday, October 11, 6 am–6 pm (final weekend: October 9–11). Earlier in the season the county extended a few evenings to 8 pm (the Sundays of Memorial Day and Labor Day weekends, and Friday, July 3) and ran on the Monday holidays and on Juneteenth.

The pickup moves with the day of the week. On Fridays the shuttle leaves from the Nederland Park-n-Ride; on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays it leaves from Nederland High School, 597 Colorado 130. Buses depart every 15 minutes, the ride takes about 10 minutes, and leashed dogs are allowed aboard. The shuttle stops only at Hessie; it does not go up to the [Fourth of July Trailhead](/places/fourth-of-july-trailhead/).

The county also points out that you do not need to drive up Boulder Canyon at all: RTD's NB bus runs hourly from Boulder to Nederland High School, with free weekend parking at Downtown Boulder Station and the Boulder County Justice Center, and connects to the shuttle there. The county lists the NB fare as $2.75 for adults, free for riders 19 and under, and $1.35 for seniors and people with disabilities.

After October 11 there is no shuttle until next season, and the county's [Hessie Trailhead page](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/parks-and-trails/hessie-trailhead/) is where next year's dates, hours and pickup points will be posted; shuttle questions go to transit@bouldercounty.gov.

### Backpackers and overnight parking

The shuttle does not run Monday to Thursday, and the county does not allow overnight parking in the high school lot. It says to park overnight on County Road 130 (Eldora Road) in front of the baseball field or the football and soccer field, not in front of the school lot, and to flag the shuttle down at the RTD stop on CR 130 across from the school.

## The trails

The county gives these round-trip distances from the shuttle drop-off, and the Forest Service lists the same trails from the trailhead:

| Destination | Round trip |
| --- | --- |
| Lost Lake | 4.2 miles |
| Woodland Lake | 9.6 miles |
| Devils Thumb | 11.6 miles |
| King Lake | 12 miles |
| Diamond Lake | 13 miles |

The Continental Divide Trail also passes through. Roughly the first mile west of the trailhead is outside the Indian Peaks Wilderness; past the boundary sign, wilderness rules apply.

## Indian Peaks Wilderness rules

From the Forest Service's [wilderness page](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/arp/recreation/indian-peaks-wilderness) and its Hessie page:

- **Dogs** on a hand-held leash at all times.
- **No campfires.** They are prohibited year-round from Hessie, and everywhere east of the Continental Divide.
- **Groups** of 12 at most, people and stock combined. Organized groups need a permit year-round, even for a day hike (the day permit is free).
- **Bear canisters** are required year-round near every trailhead in the wilderness and throughout the Diamond Lake and Jasper Lake backcountry zones, and recommended everywhere else.
- **Overnight permits** are required June 1 through September 15 for every group camping in the wilderness, one per backcountry zone, bought on Recreation.gov. The Hessie page puts the cost at $5 per group per trip; the wilderness page itemizes $11, a $6 Recreation.gov reservation fee plus the $5 Forest Service fee, both non-refundable. From September 16 to May 31 no overnight permit is needed. Groups of 8 to 12 also need a large-group day-use permit for each zone they visit in summer.
- **Camping** at least 100 feet from lakes, streams and trails; in the Diamond Lake zone, only in designated sites.
- **No bikes** or anything else motorized or mechanized; wheelchairs are exempt. Fishing follows Colorado Parks and Wildlife rules.

## Check conditions before you leave

The Forest Service's [Current Conditions page](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/arp/conditions) carries the fire danger rating for the Boulder Ranger District, any fire restrictions, and a road status table that lists Fourth of July Road as a Boulder County road and gives its open or closed status. On October 7 the district's fire danger was listed as High. In winter, the Forest Service says, access is typically from the town of Eldora. For the trails themselves, the Boulder Ranger District is at 970-295-6600.
