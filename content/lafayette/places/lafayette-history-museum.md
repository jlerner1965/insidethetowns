The museum is the Lewis House, an 1890s miner’s cottage built at the Gladstone Mine northeast of town and moved to 108 East Simpson Street between 1905 and 1908, which Elmo Lewis sold to the Lafayette Historical Society in 1976 on the condition that it become a museum of the town’s coal-mining past. It opened that year as the Lafayette Miners Museum and took its present name in 2022 to reflect a collection, now more than 4,000 objects, that covers farming, schools, home life and civic history as well as the mines. The house is on the National Register of Historic Places and stands across from the old City Hall.

Hours are Wednesday to Friday 10 am to 2 pm and Saturday 8 am to noon. The society’s site lists no admission fee. Groups are asked to call or email ahead.
