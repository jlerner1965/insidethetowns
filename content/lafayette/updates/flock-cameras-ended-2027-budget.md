The City’s statement, dated October 20 and posted October 22, says the budget "removes funding for Flock Safety" after a public hearing on October 6 at which residents spoke about the system. Between that hearing and the vote, City leadership and police command had already suspended access to Lafayette’s Flock data for outside agencies and for the City’s own public-safety staff. The steps the statement lists after the vote: end all use of Flock technologies immediately, begin early termination of the contract, cover every camera under the City’s contract until Flock removes it, and, unless Council says otherwise, sign no contract for any other automated license-plate reader. The statement credits the police department with handling the program and its data with care, and notes that ALPR cameras in the area run by corporations or neighboring municipalities are not covered by the City’s decision.
