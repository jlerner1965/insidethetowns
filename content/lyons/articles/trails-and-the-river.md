The North and South St. Vrain join in the middle of town, and the state gauge just below the confluence (station SVCLYOCO, on the [Colorado Division of Water Resources site](https://dwr.state.co.us/Tools/Stations/SVCLYOCO)) reports the flow every fifteen minutes. Read it before you plan anything on the water.

## What the number means

A rule of thumb from the outfitters and the tubing regulars, not a rule. Cubic feet per second is the gauge's unit. Children twelve and under must wear a life jacket on the river in Lyons; everyone should when it is fast.

| Flow | What it means |
|---|---|
| Under 40 cfs | Low. Too shallow for tubes; fine for wading, fishing and sitting on the whitewater-park rocks. Most of September to April. |
| 40 to 100 | Mellow. Easy tubing through LaVern M. Johnson Park with a life jacket on the kids. Late summer. |
| 100 to 300 | Prime. The classic float from the Apple Valley Road bridge down through town, with the park's drops at their best. Usually July into August. |
| 300 to 700 | Fast. Pushy water and real holes in the park; experienced floaters only, no small children. Late June and early July in a big snow year. |
| 700 to 1,200 | High. Not for tubes. Kayakers and rafters only. Peak runoff, late May to mid-June. |
| Over 1,200 | Flood watch. The Town posts advisories at this level; stay off the water and away from cut banks. |

[Tubing the St. Vrain](/places/tubing-the-st-vrain/) has the put-ins. Anglers work both forks, best in the low, clear water of late summer and fall; [Bohn Park](/places/bohn-park/) has an accessible fishing platform, and Ralph Price Reservoir in Button Rock needs a City of Longmont permit on top of the Colorado license.

## The trails

Distances and rules are from each managing agency's own page, linked from the place. Sunrise to sunset; no camping at any of them.

- **[Hall Ranch](/places/hall-ranch/)**, a mile west on Colorado 7. 13.6 miles. The Bitterbrush climb to the Nelson Loop is the Front Range's classic mountain-bike ascent and a fine hike. Bikes yes, dogs no. Two lots, full by nine on summer weekends.
- **[Heil Valley Ranch](/places/heil-valley-ranch/)**, south off US 36. 19 miles. Picture Rock from the Lyons side links to Wild Turkey and Wapiti for a long loop. No dogs, no e-bikes; on-trail only, it is elk habitat.
- **[Rabbit Mountain](/places/rabbit-mountain/)**, north-east off Colorado 66. The dry, low, early-season option with the view back to Longs Peak. Leashed dogs welcome; rattlesnakes in summer.
- **[Button Rock Preserve](/places/button-rock-preserve/)**, seven miles west at the end of County Road 80. A gentle two-mile walk along the North St. Vrain to Longmont's dam, and the Sleepy Lion loop above it. Foot only, no dogs.
- **[In town](/places/st-vrain-river-path/)**, the river path links the parks without crossing the highway. Flat, short, the right length for the evening. Leashed dogs; off-leash at Bohn Park.

## Before you go

- **Closures.** The county closes trails after storms, for prescribed burns on the Nelson Loop between January and April, and for elk and raptors in season. [County trail closures](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/parks-and-trails/trail-closures/).
- **Mud.** Riding or walking a muddy trail ruts it for the season; turn back if you are leaving prints. Rabbit Mountain dries first, Hall Ranch by late spring.
- **Weather.** Afternoon lightning most summer days from about one o'clock; start early. Chinook winds through the canyon mouth in winter.
- **Fire.** Stage 1 and 2 restrictions are common from June; check the county's current restrictions before any flame, including at the campground.
- **Parking passes.** The Town's park lots use a parking pass on busy days; non-residents buy a day or season pass. The county trailheads are free.
- **Emergencies.** Call 911. Lyons Fire runs swiftwater rescue on the St. Vrain; the Boulder County Sheriff covers the open space.
