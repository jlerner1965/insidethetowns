Boulder County runs nearly everything a Niwot walker or rider uses. The LoBo Regional Trail passes through town between Longmont and Boulder, the County's seven short Niwot trails loop off it through the neighborhoods, and three County ponds and reservoirs, with one City of Boulder preserve beside them, are a short drive. One thing to know first: the County will close the Niwot Loop Trailhead and its access trail later this winter to repair two bridges, and has not set the dates. Every rule below is from the manager's own page, read on November 11, 2026.

| Area | Manager | Dogs | Bikes, horses | Restroom |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [LoBo Regional Trail](/places/lobo-regional-trail/) | Boulder County, with the two cities | On a leash on County land | Bikes, e-bikes, horses | Lefthand Valley Grange trailhead |
| [Niwot trails](/places/niwot-loop-trail/) | Boulder County | On a leash | Bikes, e-bikes, horses | Lefthand Valley Grange trailhead |
| Twin Lakes | Boulder County | Off leash on the West Twin Lakes Trail only | Bikes, e-bikes, horses | Portable toilet in summer |
| Walden Ponds | Boulder County | On a leash | Bikes, e-bikes, horses | Two of three trailheads |
| Sawhill Ponds | City of Boulder | Hand-held leash | No bikes; horses on most trails | Yes |
| Lagerman | Boulder County | On a leash | Bikes, e-bikes, horses | Yes |

## The LoBo Regional Trail

The [LoBo](/places/lobo-regional-trail/) is about 12 miles and rated easy, running through Gunbarrel, Niwot and open space between the two cities. The County's regional trails program calls it primarily soft-surface. Its [trail page](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/parks-and-trails/lobo-trail/) shows hikers, dogs, bikes, e-bikes and horses allowed; e-bikes are barred only on a short signed stretch near Twin Lakes. It is open to commuters 24 hours a day, but trailheads close at sunset and parking is not allowed between sunset and sunrise. Several stretches use streets, and private property borders it in places; the County still lists Jay Road to Spine Road as a missing link.

The County lists six places to park: the Lefthand Valley Grange, Monarch and Niwot trailheads, the Twin Lakes trailhead, and one City lot each in Boulder and Longmont. Restrooms are at Lefthand Valley Grange and, seasonally, at the Niwot trailhead (the County's Niwot Trails page lists only the Grange one).

In Boulder, the City's [Cottonwood Trailhead](https://bouldercolorado.gov/trailhead/cottonwood), 5102 Independence Road, 0.2 mile east of the Diagonal Highway, has 16 spaces and one accessible, no restroom and no fee, and is open 5 am to 11 pm. On its 1.2-mile Cottonwood Trail a dog is on a hand-held leash unless it meets the voice-and-sight standard and wears a City of Boulder Voice and Sight tag; Class 1 and 2 e-bikes and horses are allowed. In Longmont, the City's [Left Hand Creek Neighborhood Park](https://longmontcolorado.gov/facilities/left-hand-creek-neighborhood-park/), 1880 Creekside Drive, south of Pike Road, has a lot, restrooms and a playground, open from an hour before sunrise to an hour after sunset.

## The Niwot Loop: the County's seven Niwot trails

The County's LoBo brochure marks it as the Niwot Loop Trail; its [Niwot Trails page](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/parks-and-trails/niwot-trails/) lists seven named trails, all easy: Niwot Hills 0.6 miles, Cougar 0.8, 95th Street 1.0, Somerset 1.1, Overbrook 1.3, and Hidden Star and the Legend Ridge Loop 1.5 each. Dogs go on a leash. Hikers, bikes, e-bikes and horses are allowed on all seven, though some marked sections of Hidden Star are closed to horses. The page gives no surface. The trails close at sunset; the LoBo stays open around the clock.

Three trailheads, as the County's map places them:

- **Lefthand Valley Grange**, just west of North 83rd Street, north of Niwot Road, on the LoBo: 30 spaces, two van-accessible, a restroom and a 50-person group shelter.
- **Monarch**, off Monarch Road, west of North 79th Street: 10 spaces, no designated accessible spaces, one horse-trailer space.
- **Niwot Loop**, on the east side of North 79th Street, just north of Mineral Road (Colorado 52): 13 spaces and one van-accessible.

Later this winter, crews will repair two bridges near the Niwot Loop Trailhead, and the trailhead and trail will be closed during the work. The County's closure map marks the short access trail from that lot; the recycling drop-off stays open, and dates will be announced ([the notice](/updates/#niwot-loop-trail-winter-closure)). While it is shut, start from the Grange or Monarch.

For a flat or a tune-up first, [Niwot Wheel Works](/places/niwot-wheel-works/), 7960 Niwot Road, is closed Sunday and Monday.

## A short drive

**Twin Lakes**, in Gunbarrel, sits on the LoBo, off Twin Lakes Road at Nautilus Drive on the County's map. The [County's page](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/parks-and-trails/twin-lakes/) gives two 0.7-mile easy trails: dogs on a leash on the East Twin Lakes Trail, off leash on the West Twin Lakes Trail. The regulations name only two County places where a dog may be off leash, this trail and the Forest Service trails at Reynolds Ranch, and the owner must be able to restrain the dog when staff ask. E-bikes are barred on the ditch trail between 63rd Street and Twin Lakes Road. Parking is on the street. No swimming or boating; the lakes are not stocked, and the County's fishing page says their water is unusually low.

**Walden Ponds Wildlife Habitat** is on 75th Street between Valmont and Jay roads. The [County's page](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/parks-and-trails/walden-ponds-wildlife-habitat/) gives 2.6 miles of easy trail, dogs on a leash. Cottonwood Marsh trailhead has 25 spaces, two accessible, a restroom, a 50-person shelter and two horse-trailer spaces; Wally Toevs has 13 and five accessible, with a restroom; Heatherwood has 14 and one. Fishing at Wally Toevs Pond is only for people 65 and older, people with disabilities and their companions 15 and under. Its pier is out: the closures page lists it closed for structural problems, and the Walden page says it has been removed and will be rebuilt in 2027. The County warns of low water and dead fish.

**Sawhill Ponds**, next to Walden on the County's map, is owned by Colorado Parks and Wildlife and run by the City of Boulder: 18 ponds at 3400 75th Street, 0.6 mile north of Valmont Road. The [City's page](https://bouldercolorado.gov/trailhead/sawhill-ponds) gives 27 spaces and one accessible, a restroom, a picnic pavilion, no drinking water, no fee, and hours of 5 am to 11 pm. No bicycles. Dogs stay on a hand-held leash at all times; horses are allowed on most trails.

**Lagerman Agricultural Preserve** is on Pike Road, west of North 75th Street. The [County's page](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/parks-and-trails/lagerman/) gives the 1.6-mile Lagerman Trail and the 4.9-mile Open Sky Loop, both easy, dogs on a leash, bikes, e-bikes and horses allowed. The trailhead, rebuilt by December 2025, has 24 spaces, two accessible, a restroom, a 25-person shelter and six horse-trailer spaces. The reservoir's west end closes April 1 to August 31 for nesting wildlife. The boat ramp is closed for low water, and fishing is not recommended.

## Rules on all County land

The County's [regulations](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/parks-and-trails/rules-and-regulations/): pets on a leash and under physical control, waste carried out; unattended vehicles left after dark ticketed or towed; no swimming, wading or drones; a fishing license from age 16. Fines run to $300; a ranger is at 303-441-4444. None of the County pages above lists a fee.

## Which to choose

- **A flat hour from home:** the Niwot trails, from the Grange once the Niwot Loop lot closes.
- **Riding to Boulder or Longmont:** the LoBo, with some street riding.
- **A dog off leash:** the West Twin Lakes Trail.
- **No bikes coming past:** Sawhill Ponds.
- **A wheelchair:** Walden Ponds, Twin Lakes or Lagerman, which the County lists as popular with wheelchair users.
- **A longer walk:** the Open Sky Loop at Lagerman, 4.9 miles.
