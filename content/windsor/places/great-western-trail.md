The trail follows the right-of-way of the Great Western Railroad, laid in the early 1900s for the sugar beet trade, and is built and looked after by the Great Western Trail Authority, which its About page describes as a local government body created by agreement between Windsor, Severance and Eaton. Its wide, gently graded crusher-fines surface runs about 3 miles north from the Windsor trail system into the center of Severance, then turns east for 7.5 miles through farmland to Cheyenne Avenue in Eaton, with views of the farms and the mountains to the west. The Authority's trail map page puts the Severance Trailhead on County Road 74, 1.9 miles from Severance Middle School, and marks parking and dog waste stations; its News page says the last 1.5 miles were finished in August 2023, that motor vehicles are not allowed, and that dogs must stay on a leash. Through Windsor's trail system it reaches Windsor Lake, and on-street bike lanes link it to the Poudre River Trail.

The Town's Hearts around Severance Scavenger Hunt page lists "Great Western Trail & Hidden Valley Pkwy" as one of its stops.
