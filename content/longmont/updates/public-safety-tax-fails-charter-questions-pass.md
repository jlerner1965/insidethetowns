Boulder County's unofficial count, last updated at 5:23 pm on November 4, and Weld County's summary report of 5:03 pm the same day together give: Ballot Issue 3A, the public-safety tax, 13,223 for and 24,370 against (Boulder 13,132 to 24,198, Weld 91 to 172); Ballot Question 3B, open space, 30,373 to 7,236 (Boulder 30,158 to 7,190, Weld 215 to 46); 3C, ranked-choice voting, 20,798 to 16,274 (20,658 to 16,154; 140 to 120); and 3D, council election cycles, 21,250 to 14,318 (21,110 to 14,211; 140 to 107). The questions, as the City's [ballot information page](https://longmontcolorado.gov/information/ballot-information-november-3-2026-election/) sets them out: 3A would have added 0.33 percent to the public safety sales and use tax and 2.44 mills to the property tax, about $75 a year on a $500,000 house, from 2027, to add 43 police, 31 fire and 12 collaborative-services positions over ten years and build a Fire Station 7 on the south-west side. 3B adds City-owned open space to the parks and water rights the charter says cannot be sold or conveyed without a public vote, with exceptions for public roads, utilities, life-essential infrastructure, open space operations and structures. 3C provides for ranked voting to elect the mayor and council where possible from the November 2029 election, once the Colorado Secretary of State has certified equipment and rules for it. 3D, which depended on 3C, puts the three at-large seats on one election cycle and the three ward seats on the other; to make the switch, the at-large seat elected in 2027 and the Ward 2 seat elected in 2029 serve two-year terms. The counts stay unofficial until the counties certify them.
