Boulder County's unofficial count, last updated at 12:59 am on November 4, and Weld County's summary report of 11:35 pm on November 3 together give: Ballot Issue 3A, the public-safety tax, 11,795 for and 21,003 against (Boulder 11,711 to 20,864, Weld 84 to 139); Ballot Question 3B, open space, 26,594 to 6,237 (Boulder 26,405 to 6,204, Weld 189 to 33); 3C, ranked-choice voting, 18,226 to 14,150 (18,099 to 14,057; 127 to 93); and 3D, council election cycles, 18,701 to 12,381 (18,573 to 12,299; 128 to 82). The questions, as the City's [ballot information page](https://longmontcolorado.gov/information/ballot-information-november-3-2026-election/) sets them out: 3A would have added 0.33 percent to the public safety sales and use tax and 2.44 mills to the property tax, about $75 a year on a $500,000 house, from 2027, to add 43 police, 31 fire and 12 collaborative-services positions over ten years and build a Fire Station 7 on the south-west side. 3B adds City-owned open space to the parks and water rights the charter says cannot be sold or conveyed without a public vote, with exceptions for public roads, utilities, life-essential infrastructure, open space operations and structures. 3C provides for ranked voting to elect the mayor and council where possible from the November 2029 election, once the Colorado Secretary of State has certified equipment and rules for it. 3D, which depended on 3C, puts the three at-large seats on one election cycle and the three ward seats on the other; to make the switch, the at-large seat elected in 2027 and the Ward 2 seat elected in 2029 serve two-year terms. The counts stay unofficial until the counties certify them.
