## What you are moving to

Fort Collins is a home-rule city of 169,810 people at the 2020 census, the [fourth most populous in Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Collins,_Colorado), on the Cache la Poudre River where the plains meet the foothills, [60 miles north of Denver](https://www.visitftcollins.com/maps-info/) and the last city on I-25 before Wyoming. The City's own [overview](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Uncategorized-Content/Fort-Collins-Overview) puts the population at roughly 170,000 (2023, Census Bureau), the area at 58.1 square miles, the elevation at 5,000 feet, the sunshine at 300 days a year and the median age at 30.9, which is what a university in the middle of town does: Colorado State University enrolled a record [34,412 students](https://newsmediarelations.colostate.edu/2025/10/09/all-csu-system-universities-grew-enrollment-in-fall-2025/) in fall 2025. It grew from 143,986 in 2010 to 169,810 in 2020, and it is the city the towns around it run on: the school district reaches Timnath, Wellington, Laporte and Red Feather, the fire authority covers 230 square miles, and the buses run to Loveland, Longmont and Boulder.

## The neighborhoods

- **Old Town** is the original 1873 plat north of Mountain Avenue: a 26-acre [National Register district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Fort_Collins) of 1880s and 1900s brick around Old Town Square and Linden Street, a [local historic district since 1979](https://fortcollins.gov/Services/Historic-Preservation/), the breweries on Linden and Lincoln, the Museum of Discovery, the Downtown Transit Center and the older houses on the grid around it. You get the restaurants, the music and the trolley at your door, and the smallest lots.
- **Midtown** is the three miles of College Avenue from Prospect Road south to Fairway Lane, with the MAX line down the Mason corridor behind it: a commercial strip of shopping centers and the Foothills mall site that the City's [Midtown Plan](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Government/City-Plans-and-Projects/City-Plans) (2013) describes as active, car-oriented and lacking a distinct identity, with MAX stations at Prospect, Spring Creek, Drake, Swallow, Horsetooth, Troutman and Harmony. You get a MAX station within walking distance and the middle of the city.
- **Campus West** is the commercial district on West Elizabeth Street west of CSU, which the City's [West Central Area Plan](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Government/City-Plans-and-Projects/City-Plans) (2015) calls the primary destination for eating and drinking near campus and intends as a "mini-downtown," with the single-family neighborhoods south of Elizabeth behind it. You get CSU, City Park and the foothills trails close, and students for neighbors.
- **North College** is the highway strip north of the Poudre, from the river to the "Y" where College Avenue meets Highway 1, which the [North College Corridor Plan](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Government/City-Plans-and-Projects/City-Plans) (2007) describes as the northern entryway to the city, built ad hoc before city planning, a commercial corridor for commuters and travelers and the start of the Scenic Byway to the mountains; the City has been planning it since 1994. You get the river trail and a corridor the City is still remaking.
- **Harmony** is the five-mile corridor from the I-25 interchange west to the railroad, half a mile each side of Harmony Road, which the [Harmony Corridor Plan](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Government/City-Plans-and-Projects/City-Plans) (2006) calls the city's southern entrance, the commuter route to Denver and one of its primary employment districts, carrying about 28,000 vehicles a day. You get the office parks, the Harmony Transfer Center where Bustang stops and the shortest drive to the interstate.
- **South Fort Collins** is everything between Harmony and the Loveland line: the subdivisions around Fossil Ridge High School, Fossil Creek Community Park and the Council Tree Library, with the [Fossil Creek and Power trails](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Activities/Parks-Natural-Areas-and-Trails/Trails/Paved-Trails), the Long View Trail into Loveland and the South Transit Center at the end of MAX. You get the trails and the transit center, and Loveland next door.
- **The foothills** are the west edge, where the streets run up to the hogback and stop at the City's natural areas: Pineridge, Maxwell and Reservoir Ridge, linked by the 9.6-mile [Foothills Trail](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Parks-and-Natural-Areas-Directory/Natural-Areas/Pineridge-Natural-Area), with Horsetooth Reservoir over the ridge. You get the views and the trailheads, and the longest drive to I-25.

## Housing

The City's [Housing Strategic Plan](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Government/City-Plans-and-Projects/City-Plans) of March 2021 is the plainest statement of the market. It says that since 2010 rents rose 68 percent, the median sale price of a single-family detached home rose 124 percent, from about $200,000 to $448,250, and the median for a townhome or condominium rose 164 percent, from about $120,000 to $316,885 (Fort Collins Board of Realtors, December 2020), while wages rose 25 percent; that nearly 60 percent of renters and 20 percent of homeowners are cost-burdened, spending more than 30 percent of income on housing; that the city is now almost evenly split between renters and owners; and that affordable units were 5 percent of the 70,692 homes in the city against a Council goal, set in 2015, of 10 percent by 2040. The [overview](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Uncategorized-Content/Fort-Collins-Overview) counts 74,558 housing units and a median household income of $85,070 (2024). The listings sites in the sidebar show what is for sale; prices have moved since the plan's figures, so take them as the shape of the market rather than the number. Ask whether a house is inside the City's water service area or in a water district, which changes who bills you (below).

## Schools

Fort Collins is in the [Poudre School District](https://psdschools.org/your-district), based at 2407 LaPorte Avenue, which teaches about 28,000 students across more than 1,800 square miles, from the city to Laporte, Timnath, Wellington, Red Feather, Livermore, Stove Prairie and parts of Windsor and Loveland: 31 elementary schools, 8 middle schools, 2 middle-high schools, 4 comprehensive high schools, 2 alternative high schools, 2 K-12 schools and 5 charter schools. The four comprehensive high schools are [Fort Collins, Poudre, Rocky Mountain and Fossil Ridge](https://www.psdschools.org/schools), each fed by its own middle and elementary schools. Every address has a neighborhood school, and a student enrolled there rolls over each year without paperwork; to attend any other school, or a choice-only school, you file a [School Choice](https://www.psdschools.org/our-schools/school-options-choice) application. First-round applications for 2027–28 open in early November 2026 and are decided by lottery with priority rankings; a second round opens in mid-January 2027 and runs to early May, decided by priority and the time the application was submitted. The district's choice office is at 970-490-3203.

## Getting around

[Transfort](https://ridetransfort.com/) is the City's bus system and it has been fare-free on every route since the pandemic, a decision the City Manager made [permanent in October 2024](https://ridetransfort.com/fare-free-transit-continues/). Its spine is [MAX](https://ridetransfort.com/routes/max-bus-rapid-transit-service/), the bus rapid transit line on the Mason corridor with 12 stations between the [Downtown Transit Center](https://ridetransfort.com/?p=774) at 250 North Mason Street and the South Transit Center at 4915 Fossil Boulevard, linking Old Town, CSU and Midtown, partly on its own guideway; around it run [18 routes](https://ridetransfort.com/routes-and-schedules/) including FLEX, the regional route to Loveland, Longmont and Boulder. [Bustang](https://www.codot.gov/travel/bustang), CDOT's interregional express bus, runs weekday commuter service from the Downtown Transit Center and Harmony Road to Loveland and [Denver Union Station](https://www.codot.gov/news/2019/may/bustang-adds-roundtrip-service-to-fort-collins-vail), with wifi, restrooms and bike racks. By car, I-25 is the east edge of the city and the Harmony interchange is the route to Denver; [Visit Fort Collins](https://www.visitftcollins.com/maps-info/transportation/) puts downtown Denver and Denver International Airport each at about an hour, and Cheyenne, Boulder and Estes Park at under an hour. Off the road, the City's [paved trails](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Activities/Parks-Natural-Areas-and-Trails/Trails/Paved-Trails) run 12.3 miles along the Poudre, 6.6 miles along Spring Creek, 8.5 along Fossil Creek, 4.6 beside MAX on the Mason Trail and 4 down Shields Street into Loveland, and the City publishes [bike maps](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Services/Transportation-and-Roads/Getting-Around-Town/Bicycling/Bike-Maps).

## Utilities, trash, fire, police

- **Electricity, water, wastewater and stormwater** are [Fort Collins Utilities](https://fortcollins.gov/Services/Utilities), owned by the City, serving more than 75,000 customers; start service [online](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Services/Utilities/StartStop-Service) or at 970-212-2900. Utilities supplies water, from the upper Poudre and Horsetooth Reservoir, to about [75 percent](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Services/Utilities/Other-Utilities-Pages/Water) of addresses inside the city limits; the rest are in the Fort Collins-Loveland Water District or the East Larimer County Water District, so ask before you sign.
- **Internet** is [Connexion](https://fcconnexion.com/), the City's own fiber network: symmetrical 1-gig, 2-gig and 10-gig plans at $70, $100 and $200 a month, no contract, free installation, with phone and TV options, also at 970-212-2900.
- **Trash, recycling and yard waste** are collected under a City [contract with Republic Services](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Services/Trash-and-Recycling/Curbside-Trash-and-Recycling) that began September 30, 2024, for single-family homes and buildings of seven or fewer units inside the city. You pick a cart size and pay by the month, from $11.41 for a 35-gallon cart every other week to $56.82 for a 95-gallon cart weekly; weekly recycling, yard trimmings from April to November and two bulk pickups a year are included. Sign up with Republic at 970-416-2012. Apartments and larger buildings arrange their own.
- **Police** is [Fort Collins Police Services](https://fortcollins.gov/Services/Police-and-Public-Safety/Police-Services), 236 sworn officers and 145 civilian staff under Chief Jeff Swoboda, at 2221 South Timberline Road; non-emergency 970-419-3273.
- **Fire and emergency medical** response is the [Poudre Fire Authority](https://www.poudre-fire.org/), a separate authority with, the City says, [almost 200 firefighters and 13 stations](https://www.fcgov.com/citygive/pfa-campaign) covering 230 square miles in and around the city.
- **Library** is the [Poudre River Public Library District](https://www.poudrelibraries.org/locations/), with three branches: Old Town at 201 Peterson Street, Harmony at 4616 South Shields Street and Council Tree at 2733 Council Tree Avenue; 970-221-6740.

## Government

Fort Collins adopted its home-rule charter in [October 1954](https://history.fcgov.com/timeline/1950), under Article XX of the state constitution, and has had a council-manager government since. The [City Council](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Government/City-Council/) is six members elected by district to four-year terms, limited to two, and a mayor elected at large to a two-year term, limited to three; the mayor is Emily Francis. Council holds its [regular meetings](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Government/City-Council/Council-Meetings) on the first and third Tuesdays of each month at 6 pm in the Council Chambers at City Hall, 300 LaPorte Avenue, streamed and archived on FCTV, with public comment for anyone who signs up by 5:30; work sessions are the second and fourth Tuesdays at 6, open to watch but not to speak. The [City Manager](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Government/City-Manager), Kelly DiMartino since June 2022, runs the operation of about 2,500 employees, and Fort Collins Utilities, Transfort, the Gardens and the parks are City operations, with the Museum of Discovery a partnership between the City and a nonprofit.

## Living here

Summer is the fare-free buses to the Independence Day celebration at [City Park](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Parks-and-Natural-Areas-Directory/Parks/City-Park), the lazy river at [City Park Pool](https://www.fortcollins.gov/Venues/City-Park-Pool) from late May, the swim beaches at [Horsetooth](https://www.larimer.gov/naturalresources/parks/horsetooth-reservoir) from Memorial Day weekend, the [trolley](https://history.fcgov.com/explore/trolley) on Mountain Avenue every weekend from May to September and concerts at the [Gardens on Spring Creek](https://gardens.fortcollins.gov/Home). Fall is Pumpkins on Parade at the Gardens in late October and the first storytime term at the [libraries](https://poudrelibraries.org/storytime). Winter is the Garden of Lights, and spring is the plant sale. Year-round there are the breweries that started it, [Odell](https://www.odellbrewing.com/our-story/) in 1989 and [New Belgium](https://www.newbelgium.com/company/story/) in 1991, the [Museum of Discovery](https://fcmod.org/hours-location-admission/) with its free rooftop deck, and 29 miles of trail at [Horsetooth Mountain](https://www.larimer.gov/naturalresources/parks/horsetooth-mountain) for $10 a car. What is on this week is on the [events page](/events/).
