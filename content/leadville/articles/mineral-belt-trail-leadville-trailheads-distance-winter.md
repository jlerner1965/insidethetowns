The Mineral Belt Trail is a free, paved loop of about 11.6 miles round Leadville, owned and maintained by [Lake County](https://lakecountyco.gov/382/Parks-and-Facilities). Start at the main trailhead, a dirt lot at the base of Dutch Henry Hill on U.S. 24 south of downtown, where the round metal mile markers begin and count clockwise. For a short walk, go out to mile marker 2 and back: 4 miles. The whole loop climbs 859 feet.

This is for walkers, cyclists, families with strollers and, in winter, skiers. The trail runs at over 10,000 feet. In winter it stays open, but Lake County has posted nothing yet on grooming for 2026–27.

## How far, and how steep

- **Length:** about 11.6 miles. COTREX's [loop route](https://trails.colorado.gov/routes/138) gives 11.7 miles with 859 feet of climbing, and rates it easy.
- **Height:** from 9,897 to 10,637 feet, on [COTREX](https://trails.colorado.gov/trails/mineral-belt-13514).
- **Surface:** 12 feet of pavement with 3-foot soft shoulders, an average grade of 3 percent and sustained grades of no more than 5 percent, the trail's [history page](https://www.mineralbelttrail.com/history-vision) says.
- **Who can use it:** the trail is [built for](https://www.mineralbelttrail.com/volunteer) bicycles, walkers, wheelchairs, strollers, in-line skaters and long-boarders, all year. No motor vehicles. The Tourism Panel says Class I and II e-bikes are allowed.
- **Crossings:** six road crossings and two bridges, in the County's grooming contract.

## Where to start

| Trailhead | Where | Restrooms |
|---|---|---|
| Dutch Henry | A dirt lot at the base of Dutch Henry Hill, on U.S. 24 south of downtown; the first mile marker | None |
| Ice Palace Park | 11th Street and Harrison Avenue | In season, with water |
| Lake County Community Park | 6th Street and McWethy Drive; park at Community Field or the school lots | Indoor restrooms in the park |
| East 5th Street | About 1.25 miles from Harrison Avenue: a dirt lot with historic structures, just before the metal footbridge | None |
| East 7th Street | About 1.25 miles from Harrison Avenue: a pullout on the left, under the metal footbridge | None |

The restrooms are from COTREX's trailhead pages and the County's [Community Park page](https://www.lakecountyco.gov/facilities/facility/details/Community-Park-Fairgrounds-3); none of the trailheads charges a fee. The [Tourism Panel](https://www.leadvilletwinlakes.com/trail/mineral-belt-trail/) lists two more lots: a pullout on County Road 2 at California Gulch, just before the trail crossing, and a dirt lot on County Road 6, Landfill Road, before the landfill entrance. To reach Dutch Henry, drive south past the entrance to Colorado Mountain College and turn left into the dirt lot where the trail crosses the road.

## On the trail

- **Mile markers** count clockwise. The pavement carries [colored marks](https://www.mineralbelttrail.com/thetrail): in an emergency, give the color or the nearest marker.
- **Shelters** stand every few miles; the Tourism Panel puts them after mile markers 3, 5, 7 and 10.
- **History:** the trail follows California Gulch, where Leadville's first gold was found, along parts of three old rail lines, with signs on the Tabors, Meyer Guggenheim, Molly Brown and others.
- **Stay on the pavement:** beside it are historic structures, open mine shafts and private land.

## Dogs

- **On a leash,** under county law. Lake County's [animal ordinance](https://www.lakecountyco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1954/2008-01-Lake-County-Animal-Control-and-Licensing-Requirements-Ordinance-of-2008-which-Provides-for-the-Control-and-Licensing-of-Pet-Animals-The) means a leash or tether no longer than 10 feet, held by the owner, and you pick up the waste straight away.
- **Off the leash:** the County's dog park, 505 West 5th Street, open dawn to dusk all year.
- **Ice Palace Park** is a dog-free park, the [Tourism Panel](https://www.leadvilletwinlakes.com/listing/ice-palace-park/) says, so start elsewhere with a dog.

## In winter

The County [grooms the trail](https://lakecountyco.gov/382/Parks-and-Facilities) for Nordic skiing and snowshoeing, and the trail's site adds winter biking. It is free.

- **This winter:** nothing posted yet. The County's [Winter Groomed Trails](https://www.lakecountyco.gov/630/Winter-Groomed-Trails) page still carries last season's notice: grooming ended early after an exceptionally low snowfall, and the trails stay open for use at your own discretion. The last report on [Nordic Pulse](https://nordic-pulse.com/ski-areas/US/CO/Leadville-Town-Trails) is from March 7, 2026, and no 2026–27 grooming contract is [among the County's bids](https://www.lakecountyco.gov/bids.aspx?showAllBids=on).
- **Last season's rules:** the County's [2025–26 contract](https://lakecountyco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/2348/Winter-Trail-Grooming-RFP-2025-26---Final) ran grooming from December 1 to March 31, with the first full groom at about six inches of snow, usually on Thursday and Monday nights between 10 pm and 8 am. Each groom makes one skate lane and one classic track. The snowcat cannot cross the bridges.
- **Dutch Henry Tubing Hill,** above the main trailhead, [will not open](https://www.lakecountyco.gov/379/Dutch-Henry-Tubing-Hill) for 2026–27.
- **The Leadville Loppet,** a 5, 10, 22 and 44 km ski race on the trail, is held on the last Saturday of February; 2027's details are not yet published.

## Two ways to do it

1. **A short walk:** from Dutch Henry, follow the mile markers to marker 2 and turn back, 4 miles.
2. **The whole loop:** 11.6 miles and 859 feet of climbing, on foot or by bike, with the shelters as rest stops.

## At 10,000 feet

The Forest Service's [advice for Leadville's peaks](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/psicc/recreation/mount-elbert-fourteener) holds on the trail: acclimate gradually and drink plenty. If a severe headache, dizziness or nausea starts, go down to a lower elevation. The sun is much stronger and the air drier, so wear sunscreen and a hat.
