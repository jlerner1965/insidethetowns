Castle Rock's outings for children gather in three places: downtown, where the library, the depot museum and the holiday events are; Philip S. Miller Park, home of The Edge; and the County's land at the fairgrounds and out on the open space. One gap first. The Town of Castle Rock runs its own parks and recreation, and its website, crgov.com, could not be read for this guide, so the Town's parks, pools and recreation centers have no hours or prices here; check them there. The rest follows, with the hours, ages and prices each operator publishes.

## The library: storytime and The Ranch

The [Philip & Jerry Miller Library](/places/castle-rock-library/) at 100 S. Wilcox Street (303-791-7323) is open 8 to 8 Monday to Thursday, 8 to 6 on Friday and 10 to 4 at the weekend ([Douglas County Libraries](https://www.dcl.org/castle-rock/)). Its play space, [The Ranch](https://www.dcl.org/playscapes/), has a standalone stable, a tractor and a mini grain silo, with play materials for tending horses and animals, baling hay and gardening; the Rotary Club of Castle Rock sponsors it.

[Storytime](https://www.dcl.org/storytime/) runs Monday to Saturday across the library district, with songs, rhymes, reading and fingerplays, and the library asks families to keep it allergen-free. On the Castle Rock [storytime calendar](https://go.dcl.org/events?et=storytime&l=castle+rock&r=months&n=1) this fall, all in the Kids Corner:

- Baby storytime (ages 0 to 2) at 9 am and toddler storytime at 10 am, Monday to Friday.
- Preschool storytime (ages 3 to 5) at 11 am, Monday to Thursday.
- Storytime Discover, with art, music, dance, yoga or the storyteller's choice, at 11 am on Friday.
- All-ages storytime at 6:30 pm on Monday, 4:30 pm on Wednesday and 10:30 am on Saturday.

For older children, [Slick Science](https://www.dcl.org/parents/) brings rotating hands-on science activities for ages 8 to 12 each spring and fall; at Castle Rock the October session, on catapults, is on [Tuesday, October 20, from 5 to 6](https://go.dcl.org/event/15771272), with registration for each child. Bark for Books lets young readers read aloud to trained therapy dogs. Battle of the Books, which the library calls a bowl-style trivia tournament about books, is for grades 3 to 6 and is entered through schools; the Castle Rock rounds [begin on January 26](https://go.dcl.org/event/17383119). The library's page for parents also points to Dolly Parton's Imagination Library, which sends children from birth to age 5 a free book each month to keep, and among the [Library Perks](https://www.dcl.org/library-perks/) are a limited number of backpacks holding a pass good for admission to any Colorado state park. Noche de Familia, an evening of storytime, snacks and crafts in English and Spanish, comes round on [various dates](https://www.dcl.org/featured-events/), and [Camp DCL](https://www.dcl.org/camp-dcl/) returns next summer.

## The Edge, in Philip S. Miller Park

[The Edge Ziplines & Adventures](/places/the-edge-ziplines/), established in 2014, is inside Philip S. Miller Park at 1375 West Plum Creek Parkway (720-733-9477). Each of its four activities has its own age and weight rules, and the operator's own pages do not always agree:

- The [zipline tour](https://theedgezip.com/ziplines): ten lines and 1.5 miles of cable in two to three hours, for ages 7 and up weighing 70 to 250 pounds, from $99.
- [Sky Trek](https://theedgezip.com/sky-trek), an aerial obstacle course: ages 4 and up, with a kids' course for ages 4 to 6, from $59. The [visitor guide](https://theedgezip.com/plan-your-visit) gives its weight range as 35 to 250 pounds; the Sky Trek page gives a maximum of 300.
- The [Adventure Tower](https://theedgezip.com/adventure-tower): one to two hours of climbing, 44 to 270 pounds, from $49; the [winter page](https://theedgezip.com/seasonal/winter-wonderland) describes a 50-foot wall and a free-fall plunge. The tower's own page says ages 4 and up; the visitor guide sets no minimum age.
- The [Ninja Warrior Course](https://theedgezip.com/ninja-warrior-course): obstacles at three levels and a 14-foot warped wall, in one-hour sessions, with no minimum age and a weight range of 30 to 270 pounds, from $29.

Everyone needs closed-toe shoes and long hair tied back, phones stay behind during the activities, and The Edge asks you to arrive 30 minutes early to check in. It operates in light rain and down to 35°F and closes for lightning and sustained winds over 25 mph. Its listed hours are 9 to 6 daily; the winter page gives weekend hours of 10 to 4, with some activities on a modified schedule, so check when you book.

The Edge's visitor guide describes Philip S. Miller Park as 300 acres, with playgrounds for younger children, hiking and walking trails, picnic areas and the Challenge Hill staircase of more than 200 steps, and free parking a short walk from The Edge's entrance.

## Fairgrounds Regional Park and the open space

Douglas County's [Fairgrounds Regional Park](/places/fairgrounds-regional-park/), 500 Fairgrounds Road, has a playground, a fitness court, six picnic shelters, restrooms, drinking fountains, ball fields where youth football, baseball and soccer are played, a dog off-leash area and 2.66 miles of paved trail, with a trail on into downtown and a view of the Rock ([Douglas County](https://www.douglasco.gov/parks-trails-building-grounds/parks-trails/fairgrounds-regional-park/)). It is open from an hour before sunrise to an hour after sunset, later for night games on the lighted fields, and groups of 15 or more should reserve. Next door, the [Douglas County Fairgrounds](https://www.douglasco.gov/county-fair-grounds/) host more than 800 events a year and offer open riding in the indoor arena through the year; the 2026 Douglas County Fair & Rodeo ran from July 24 to August 2.

For a first hike, the County's [Glendale Farm](/places/glendale-farm-open-space/) trail at 12300 S. Havana Street is a moderately easy 1.6-mile loop, with a spur to the rocks, that climbs about 200 feet through the oaks to the high prairie and caprock overlooks ([Douglas County](https://www.douglasco.gov/open-space-natural-resources/properties/glendale-farm-open-space-trail-and-dog-park/)). Dogs must be leashed on the trail, beside a 17-acre dog park that the County calls its busiest; there is a portable restroom and picnic tables, but no water. [Dawson Butte](/places/dawson-butte-open-space/), 1753 Tomah Road, is a longer outing: a 5-mile loop through forest and meadow, plus 2.7 miles of horse paths, shared with riders and mountain bikers. There is a restroom but no water, dogs must be leashed, and the County reminds visitors that elk, bears and mountain lions live there ([Douglas County](https://www.douglasco.gov/open-space-natural-resources/properties/dawson-butte-open-space-and-trail/)). Both are open from an hour before sunrise to an hour after sunset.

## The depot museum

The [Castle Rock Museum](/places/castle-rock-museum/) is in the town's 1875 railroad depot at 420 Elbert Street, open Wednesday to Friday from noon to 5 and Saturday from 11 to 4 ([Historical Society](https://www.castlerockhistoricalsociety.org/?page_id=3044)). On Saturday, December 12, from 10 am to noon, Santa visits the museum, with complimentary hot chocolate and cookies; bring your own camera ([events](https://www.castlerockhistoricalsociety.org/?page_id=3208)). The Historical Society has put markers with QR codes on historic buildings around town, and anyone who finds them all and tells the museum gets a certificate of completion; its [page](https://www.castlerockhistoricalsociety.org/local-information/) gives the number of buildings as both 24 and 21. Its Ghostly Stories and Tragic Tales walking tours in October are free with a reservation, and they tell of ghostly and tragic events, so decide for your own children.

## Fall and the holidays

Through October the Outlets at Castle Rock show off [Big Jack](https://landing.outletsatcastlerock.com/BigJack), which they call Colorado's largest pumpkin, and run [Big Jack's Great Hide-and-Seek Adventure](https://www.outletsatcastlerock.com/event/44153-big-jacks-great-hide-and-seek-adventure) for children 10 and under: pick up a map at Customer Service, find the twelve hobbit friends hiding in store windows, and bring the finished map back for a prize. The rest of the season, with dates:

- **Saturday, October 24, 11 to 2:** the Outlets' [Boo Bash](https://www.outletsatcastlerock.com/event/44220-boo-bash), with a petting zoo, face painting and a costume contest by age group from noon.
- **Friday, October 30, 11 to 2:** the Downtown Alliance's [Fangtastic Festival](https://www.downtowncastlerock.com/fangtasticfestival) in Festival Park, a daytime booth-to-booth trick-or-treat for homeschooled children and little ones not yet in school.
- **Saturday, November 21, 2 to 6:** the Chamber's [Starlighting](https://castlerock.org/castle-rock-starlighting/) downtown, free, with Santa and Mrs. Claus in Festival Park from 2 to 4:15, a kids' zone with a petting zoo, face painting and games, and the star on the Rock lit at about 5:30, followed by fireworks.
- **Thanksgiving morning, November 26:** the [Turkey ROCK Trot](https://www.douglasco.gov/fairground-events/turkey-rock-trot-11262026/) 5K from the fairgrounds, with a free 1K Kids Dash, a bounce house and face painting.
- **Sunday, November 29, at 2:** the library's [Jingle Jam Sing-Along](https://go.dcl.org/event/17471546), a storytime-like holiday sing-along with live music, best for ages 2 to 5; register each guest.
- **Thursday, December 10:** [A Visit with Santa](https://go.dcl.org/event/14463389) at the library at 10, 2 and 6, with stories, activities and snacks; register each child from November 10.
- **Mondays and Tuesdays, November 23 to December 29, 5:30 to 7:30 pm:** the [free winter trolley](https://www.downtowncastlerock.com/wintertrolleyrides) loops past the holiday lights from the Encore garage on South Street; every stop takes strollers and wheelchairs.
- **Wednesday to Sunday, November 22 to January 3:** [carriage rides](https://www.downtowncastlerock.com/holidaycarriagerides) from Festival Park, $59 for a carriage of four, or in some cases two adults and three children under 10. There are 560 rides this season and the Alliance warns that not everyone who wants one will get one; 75 tickets are sold in person at the library at 4 pm on Thursday, October 29, and the rest online at noon on Wednesday, November 18.

Upcoming family events with published dates are on the guide's [events page](/events/).
