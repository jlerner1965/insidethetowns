Estes Park's places for children split between the national park on the town's west edge and the recreation district's buildings and parks in town. By November the summer half of the list has closed: the park's Junior Ranger headquarters, the tram, the marina and Trail Ridge Road over the top. The indoor pools, the library, the playgrounds and, once there is snow, the park's sledding hill carry the winter. Here is what there is, with the hours, ages and prices each operator publishes, read on November 11, 2026.

## Rocky Mountain National Park with children

The park's free [Junior Ranger](https://www.nps.gov/romo/learn/kidsyouth/junior-ranger.htm) program is the place to start. Pick up a free activity booklet at any visitor center, in English or Spanish, in three versions: ages 5 and under, 6 to 8, and 9 and up; children are sworn in and then work through it. The booklets can also be printed at home from the same page. The park's Junior Ranger Headquarters at Hidden Valley, about 7 miles up Trail Ridge Road from the Beaver Meadows or Fall River entrances, and the activities at the Moraine Park Discovery Center are closed for the season; the park suggests both places for families to explore on their own in the meantime.

On the east side, the Beaver Meadows Visitor Center on US 36 and the Fall River Visitor Center on US 34 are both [open year-round](https://www.nps.gov/romo/learn/kidsyouth/index.htm), with restrooms, an information desk and Junior Ranger booklets; Fall River has wildlife exhibits, and Beaver Meadows shows the park's film. Their [winter hours](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/visitorcenters.htm), through May 23, are 9 am to 4:30 pm daily, and both close on Thanksgiving and Christmas Day. [Ranger-led programs](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/ranger-led-programs.htm) are free and listed on the park calendar and at the visitor centers.

[Entrance](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/fees.htm) is $30 for a car for one day or $35 for seven, paid by card at the entrance stations; anyone on foot or by bike pays $15 a day, but children under 16 need no pass of their own. US fourth graders can get a free America the Beautiful pass. Visitors who live outside the United States pay an extra $100 each from age 16, unless they enter on an annual or America the Beautiful pass. Timed entry is over for the year: reservations were needed until October 12, and until October 18 on Bear Lake Road ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/timed-entry-permit-system.htm)). Trail Ridge Road is [closed to through travel](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/road_status.htm) at Many Parks Curve, as it is each year from about mid-October to late May ([hours and seasons](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/hours.htm)). Bear Lake Road stays open all year; expect snow and ice on it, and the state traction law can go into effect at any time.

Two short walks suit small legs and wheels. The loop around [Sprague Lake](/places/sprague-lake-trailhead/) on Bear Lake Road, open year-round, is wheelchair and stroller accessible, with benches, lookouts, 27 picnic tables and a vault toilet open all year; the park gives it as [0.8 mile](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/exploring-the-bear-lake-road-corridor.htm) on one page and [0.7 mile](https://www.nps.gov/places/sprague-lake.htm) on another. The [Lily Lake](/places/lily-lake-trailhead/) trail off Highway 7, which the park lists as [accessible](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/lily-lake-trail-wedding-site.htm), has benches, a viewing dock, two bridges and a boardwalk through a wetland, with vault toilets at the lot. From October to early June the park [expects](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/plan-your-winter-springtime-visit-to-rocky.htm) snow-packed or icy trails in most areas and advises traction devices.

## Elk, and the rule of thumb

The [Park Service's elk rules](https://www.nps.gov/romo/learn/nature/elk.htm) are worth teaching children before you go. Keep at least 75 feet, about two bus lengths, from elk and bighorn sheep. The test is the "rule of thumb": hold out your arm with your thumb up, close one eye, and if your thumb does not hide the animal, step back until it does. Feeding or harassing wildlife is illegal. In winter, the page says, the herds move down to the lower elevations of the park and into the Front Range. During the fall mating season the park closes off-trail walking in Horseshoe Park, Moraine Park, Upper Beaver Meadows, Harbison Meadow and Holzwarth Meadow from 5 pm to 10 am; the page does not say when that ends.

For young anglers, [anyone 16 or older needs a Colorado fishing license](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/fishing.htm) in the park, and children 12 and under may use worms or preserved fish eggs in park waters that are open to fishing and not catch-and-release. Ice fishing is allowed in the park when the ice allows, except in closed waters.

## The community center and its pools

The Estes Valley Recreation and Park District's [Estes Valley Community Center](/places/estes-valley-community-center/), at 660 Community Drive (970-586-8191), has indoor pools, a gymnasium and a track. Its [own page](https://evrpd.com/community-center/) gives two sets of hours, Monday to Friday 6 am to 8 pm in both, with Saturday 9 am to 6 pm and Sunday noon to 5 pm in one and both weekend days 9 to 6 in the other, so call before a Sunday morning swim. Children 3 and under need an adult in the water within arm's reach, children 8 and under need a parent or guardian in the pool area, and children must be 9 to be unsupervised in the pools, lobby, track and gym.

The [aquatics center](https://evrpd.com/aquatics/) has a lap pool, a dive well, a leisure pool with a lazy river and a hot tub. Open swim in the leisure pool is 11 am to 1 pm on weekdays, 5 to 6:30 pm on Monday and Wednesday, 4 to 6:30 pm on Friday, 1 to 5 pm on Saturday and 10 am to 5 pm on Sunday; the dive well has open swim 11 to 1 on weekdays and 10 to 5 on Sunday. The slide takes swimmers 42 inches and taller. The lap pool is closed for renovations, and the district gives no reopening date. It also runs Red Cross swim lessons and a rec swim team.

A [day visit](https://evrpd.com/community-center-memberships/) is $6 for residents aged 3 to 17 and $8 for adults; for non-residents, $11 and $15. A week's family membership, for up to two adults and three children under 18 at one address, is $65 for residents and $115 for others.

For ages 4 to 6, [Cubz Den](https://evrpd.com/cubz-den/) is the district's child-watch program, 3 to 5:30 pm in six-week sessions on Tuesday and Thursday or Monday, Wednesday and Friday, with an indoor playground, crafts, music and dancing. The district's [youth sports](https://evrpd.com/youth-sports-and-activities/) are open to non-members through a CivicRec account. This season's schedule has Jr. Nuggets basketball for grades 3 to 6 through December 12, gymnastics for ages 5 to 10 on Tuesdays through December 22, wrestling for ages 7 to 12 on Thursdays and Fridays from 5:15 to 6:30 pm through December 18, and Friday Night Lights for grades 1 to 6 at Stanley Park through November 20.

## Stanley Park and the Lake Estes Trail

[Stanley Park](https://evrpd.com/stanley-park/), nearly 100 acres beside Lake Estes, has a playground, always open, with slides, forts, rope walls, a zip line and swings, plus the Lawson Skatepark, a dirt mountain bike track for all ages and abilities, ballfields and courts. The first phase of its parking lot project is done, and the lot reopened on November 7; the district [says](https://evrpd.com/parks-and-trails/) it will not be plowed this winter, to protect the new pavement, and asks drivers to watch for a temporary stop sign and closed south entrances.

The [Lake Estes Trail](/places/lake-estes-trail/) is a paved, easy 4-mile loop around the lake, reached from the Visitor Center, the parking garage and the Fisherman's Nook lot, or from paid lots at Cherokee Draw, Wapiti Meadows and the marina, where a one-day vehicle permit is $8 ([marina](https://evrpd.com/marina/)). Restrooms and drinking fountains are at Cherokee Draw, and dogs on leash are welcome.

## Winter: sledding, the pools, the library and the holidays

**Sledding.** The Hidden Valley sledding hill, along Trail Ridge Road 7 miles from the Beaver Meadows and Fall River entrances, is the only place in the national park where sledding is allowed. The park's [winter page](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/winter-recreation.htm), last updated in March, calls it a gentle hill: you walk your sled up, there is no tow and nothing to rent in the park, sleds with metal runners are not allowed, pets stay off the hill, and it is unstaffed. Bring plastic sleds, saucers or tubes from town. It lists flush restrooms at the bottom of the hill, by the parking area, and a warming house that may open for limited hours on weekends. Check that page for snow before you drive up.

**Ranger walks.** Last winter's east-side ranger programs, among them kid-friendly snowshoe walks at Sprague Lake on Saturday afternoons, ran from late January to April, with reservations through the Rocky Mountain Conservancy and no snowshoes provided ([winter programs](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/winter-programs-east-side-near-estes-park-co.htm)). The park has not posted this winter's schedule. Old Fall River Road, closed to cars, is [open for the winter](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/road_status.htm) to walkers, bicycles and leashed pets, which stay on the road.

**Indoors.** The community center closes for Thanksgiving on November 26 and 27, for Christmas Eve and Christmas Day, and on New Year's Day, and closes at 5 pm on December 9 and December 31. Its hot tub shuts when the outside temperature is 20 degrees or lower; children 6 and under are not allowed in it, and those 7 to 13 need an adult. At the [library](/places/estes-valley-library/), [Baby Storytime](https://calendar.estesvalleylibrary.org/event/17818699) is at 10 am and [Storytime](https://calendar.estesvalleylibrary.org/event/17818701), for children 6 and under with a grown-up, at 10:30, on Thursdays and Saturdays in the Wasson Board Room. The library closes November 26 and 27, December 24 and 25, and January 1.

**The Polar Express.** The library reads [The Polar Express](https://calendar.estesvalleylibrary.org/event/15555638) at Town Hall on December 4, 5 and 6, at 5:30 and 6:20 pm, followed by a trolley ride to see the lights, then cocoa, cookies and a gift. Families request tickets from November 6 to November 20, and the library asks for no more than two adults per child. [Holiday Card Making](https://calendar.estesvalleylibrary.org/event/15447543), for families, is in the Makerspace on Saturday, December 12, from 11 am to noon.

**The Town's holiday events.** The free [Lighting of the Trees](/events/tree-lighting/) is in [Bond Park](/places/bond-park/) on Saturday, November 21, from 3:30 to 6 pm, with candy canes, ornament-making, Santa and Mrs. Claus, carriage rides and an all-ages electric train, the trees lit at 5:30 ([Town events](https://eventsinestes.com/tree-lighting/)). The free [Catch the Glow Parade](/events/catch-the-glow-parade/), more than 40 entries, comes down Elkhorn Avenue at 5:30 pm on Friday, November 27, the day after Thanksgiving ([Town events](https://eventsinestes.com/catch-the-glow-parade/)).

## More from the library

Most of the library's other children's programs need registration. [Storybook Explorers](https://calendar.estesvalleylibrary.org/event/17019228), for ages 0 to 5, pairs a book with a craft at the library. At the YMCA of the Rockies' Mootz Craft and Design Center, [Tiny Tots Crafts](https://calendar.estesvalleylibrary.org/event/17253159) is for ages 3 to 5 with a caregiver, and [Friday Kids Club](https://calendar.estesvalleylibrary.org/event/17674968), for ages 6 to 12, makes crafts; the November 13 session was full, with a waiting list. [Teen & Tween Cooking & Culture](https://calendar.estesvalleylibrary.org/event/17290702), for ages 10 to 18, cooks a different country's food at the community center; November's Thanksgiving session is fully booked, and December 16 is Russian dumplings. [Tween Dragon Slayers](https://calendar.estesvalleylibrary.org/event/15412968), Dungeons & Dragons for 10 to 13 at the Estes Park Memorial Observatory, is full; the teen librarian is taking names by email for a possible second group. The library's other one-off workshops for children and teens are on the [events page](/events/).

## Closed for the winter

The [Estes Park Aerial Tramway](/places/estes-park-aerial-tramway/) ended its 2026 season on November 1; it generally runs from mid-May, and this year's tickets were $24.95 for ages 6 to 17, free for 5 and under and $39.95 for adults ([tram FAQ](https://estestram.com/faq)). The [Lake Estes Marina](https://evrpd.com/marina/) store has closed for the season; its posted season starts on April 1, with weekend hours only until May 21, weather permitting. Swimming and ice fishing are not allowed on Lake Estes. Larimer County's [Hermit Park Open Space](https://www.larimer.gov/naturalresources/parks/hermit-park), 2 miles southeast of town off US 36, is 1,362 acres of ponderosa pine and wetland meadow with cabins, camping and trails for hiking, riding and mountain biking; it needs an entrance permit, is open until December 20, and closes from December 21 to the end of February, weather permitting. The [MacGregor Ranch](https://www.macgregorranch.org/) has closed tours of its family homestead museum until next summer, as have the park's Junior Ranger Headquarters and the Moraine Park Discovery Center.

The Town of Estes Park's own website, which covers its museum and visitor center, and the YMCA of the Rockies' site refused automated reads, so they are not covered here.
