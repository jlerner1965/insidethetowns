[Rocky Mountain National Park](/places/rocky-mountain-national-park/) begins at the west edge of Estes Park, with the Beaver Meadows entrance on US 36 and the Fall River entrance on US 34. A first visit by car has three things to sort out: a park pass, needed every hour of the year; from late May to mid-October, a timed-entry reservation for certain hours; and a plan for parking, since the Bear Lake Road lots fill early. Here is how it worked in 2026, read from the park's pages on October 7, 2026.

## The entrance pass

A pass is [required 24 hours a day](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/fees.htm), including at outlying areas such as Lily Lake, Longs Peak, Lumpy Ridge and Wild Basin. A private vehicle pays $30 for one day or $35 for seven consecutive days, covering everyone in the car. Anyone 16 or older arriving on foot or by bicycle pays $15 for a day or $20 for seven; children under 16 need no pass. The park's annual pass is $70, and the America the Beautiful passes are accepted. Non-US residents aged 16 and over pay an additional $100 a person unless they hold an annual or America the Beautiful pass.

The park is cashless: entrance stations take cards only, and visitors with cash can buy most passes at the Nature Store in the Beaver Meadows or Fall River visitor centers. Passes sold on Recreation.gov are non-refundable; if unsure which to buy, the park says, wait and ask at the gate.

## Timed entry in 2026

In 2026 the park ran two kinds of reservation, both sold only through [Recreation.gov](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/timed-entry-permit-system.htm) (the website, the app or 877-444-6777), never at the gates, for a non-refundable $2 fee on top of the entrance pass.

| Reservation | Where it lets you go | Hours it was needed in 2026 | Dates in 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timed Entry | Everywhere except the Bear Lake Road corridor: Trail Ridge Road, the Alpine Visitor Center, the west side, Wild Basin, Lily Lake, Lumpy Ridge, Longs Peak | 9 am–2 pm | May 22–October 12 |
| Timed Entry + Bear Lake Road | The Bear Lake Road corridor (Moraine Park, Sprague Lake, Park & Ride, Glacier Gorge, Bear Lake) and every other area | 5 am–6 pm | May 22–October 18 |

Outside those hours no reservation was needed. Each one gave a two-hour window to enter, after which you could stay as long as you liked; a Bear Lake Road holder who arrived late, or left the corridor and wanted to return, was let in again only after 2 pm. One reservation covered one vehicle for one day, the person named on it had to be present with photo ID, and it could not be transferred. Reservations were released on the first of each month at 8 am for the month ahead, with 40 percent of each day's supply held back until 7 pm the evening before. None guaranteed a parking space.

Those are the 2026 dates and hours. The park posts the following year's rules on its [timed-entry page](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/timed-entry-permit-system.htm); the 2026 ones should not be assumed to carry over.

## The shuttles

Two free [in-park shuttles](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/shuttle-buses-and-public-transit.htm) ran daily from May 22 to October 18, 2026, both from Park & Ride, the lot 5.2 miles up Bear Lake Road that is the largest on the road and open all year. The Bear Lake Route ran 6:30 am–7:30 pm every 10 to 15 minutes to the Bierstadt Lake, Glacier Gorge and Bear Lake trailheads; the Moraine Park Route ran 7 am–7:30 pm hourly to Sprague Lake, the campgrounds and the Cub Lake and Fern Lake trailheads. There is no shuttle anywhere else in the park, none over Trail Ridge Road, and none in winter.

The Hiker Shuttle from the Estes Park Visitor Center to Park & Ride ran daily from May 22 to September 7, 2026, then Saturdays and Sundays only from September 12 to October 18, with eight departures from 8:30 am to 1:30 pm and the last bus back at 6 pm. Every passenger, infants included, needed a $2 Recreation.gov reservation (up to four tickets) and a park pass. No bicycles and no pets are carried on any park bus.

## Bear Lake Road and the short walks

[Bear Lake Road](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/exploring-the-bear-lake-road-corridor.htm) turns off US 36 about 0.2 mile inside the Beaver Meadows entrance and climbs 9.2 paved miles from 8,200 feet to 9,475 feet. The park's distances for the walks that suit a first day:

| Walk | Start | Distance | Elevation gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sprague Lake loop | [Sprague Lake](/places/sprague-lake-trailhead/) | 0.8 mile loop (0.7 on the park's Sprague Lake page) | flat; wheelchair and stroller accessible |
| Bear Lake loop | [Bear Lake](/places/bear-lake-trailhead/) | 0.6 mile loop | 71 feet |
| Nymph Lake | Bear Lake | 0.5 mile one way | 231 feet |
| Dream Lake | Bear Lake | 1.1 miles one way | 474 feet |
| Emerald Lake | Bear Lake | 1.7 miles one way | 771 feet |
| Alberta Falls | [Glacier Gorge](/places/glacier-gorge-trailhead/) | 0.8 mile one way | 233 feet |

Sprague Lake, at 8,701 feet, has 27 picnic tables and a vault toilet open all year. The Bear Lake lot, the park says, is often full by 9 am every day of the year, and Glacier Gorge fills early too; its advice for both is Park & Ride. From late October to early June expect snow on the Bear Lake trail and bring traction devices.

## Altitude and weather

Bear Lake is at 9,475 feet. The park's [high country hazards](https://www.nps.gov/romo/hicountry_hazards.htm) page says high elevation increases the chance of dehydration, severe sunburn and mountain sickness (headaches, nausea, dizziness); drink several quarts of water a day and go down if you feel ill. For lightning, start early and be below treeline before a storm. The park's [weather page](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/all-about-weather.htm), with National Weather Service forecasts for Bear Lake and Trail Ridge Road, describes warm sunny mornings and afternoons that rain or snow. Above treeline it is normally windy and [20 to 30 degrees colder](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/trail_ridge_road.htm) than Estes Park, and from October through late May the park says to [expect winter conditions](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/safety-wellness.htm) at elevation.

## No pets on trails

Pets are [prohibited on all trails, tundra and meadows](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/pets.htm), in visitor centers and on the shuttles. On a leash no longer than six feet they may go along roads and in parking areas, campgrounds and picnic areas. The park's own list of nearby trails for dogs includes [Lily Mountain](/places/lily-mountain-trail/) and [Lion Gulch](/places/lion-gulch-trailhead/).

## Trail Ridge Road

[Trail Ridge Road](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/trail_ridge_road.htm), US 34 across the park, runs 48 miles from Estes Park to Grand Lake with 11 miles above treeline and a high point of 12,183 feet; allow at least half a day. It is [closed to through travel from mid-October to late May](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/hours.htm). On October 6, 2026 the park's [roads page](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/road_status.htm) still listed it open, warning that weather closures can come at any time. Bear Lake Road stays open all year.

## Where to check before you go

Road status is not posted on the park's website: use the recorded Trail Ridge Road line, 970-586-1222, the message boards in the park and @RockyNPS. The [current conditions page](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/conditions.htm) links the [trail conditions report](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/trail_conditions.htm), wildlife closures and fire information. The Information Office answers 970-586-1206, 8 am–4 pm daily in summer, and in winter 8 am–4 pm on weekdays and 8 am–noon at weekends.
