Grand Lake sits on the shore of the lake at the west entrance to Rocky Mountain National Park, which the Town says borders it to the north and east. Downtown is the boardwalk on Grand Avenue, with Lake Avenue along the north shore. Park once: the Town lists public parking at [Gene Stover Lakefront Park](/places/gene-stover-lakefront-park/), 1101 Lake Avenue, with public restrooms beside the picnic pavilions. Everything below is on foot from there, except the last stop's second restaurant; the national park is a separate plan, at the end.

Much of the town keeps a summer season. The two museums open from Memorial Day through August, with some September weekend days, and the snack bar on Lake Avenue from May to September. The shops, cafés and restaurants below post no seasonal closing, and Sagebrush and Never Summer say they are open year-round.

## 01 · The Hub

[The Hub](/places/the-hub/) at 830 Grand Avenue is open daily 7 am–3 pm, but its kitchen closes at 2:30 pm, which is why it is first. It makes coffee and breakfast burritos (bacon, sausage, egg and potato, or chicken chorizo), soup, and locally sourced bagels and pastries, and serves beer and wine.

*Getting there:* on foot along Grand Avenue. *Best for:* breakfast, or a coffee before the shops open.

## 02 · Bob Scott's and Never Summer

[Bob Scott's Authentic Indian Jewelry](/places/bob-scotts-authentic-indian-jewelry/) at 912 Grand Avenue opens daily 9 am–5 pm. It sells Native American jewelry and gifts chosen by Bob Scott, and lately antique and Western collectibles, from 1920s concho belts to a carbide miner's lantern; the stock changes week to week. Across the street at 919 Grand, [Never Summer Mountain Products](/places/never-summer-mountain-products/) has sold outdoor clothing, boots and camping gear since 1982 and rents snowshoes, backcountry skis and bear canisters; it opens at 10 am daily and closes at 5 or 6 pm, every day but Easter, Thanksgiving and Christmas.

*Getting there:* on foot along Grand Avenue. *Best for:* an hour on the boardwalk, and anything forgotten for a hike.

## 03 · Town Square Park

[Town Square Park](/places/town-square-park/) at 1025 Grand Avenue, in the middle of the boardwalk, was set aside for public use when the town was platted in 1905. It has a lawn, a gazebo, a playground and basketball court, and public restrooms, with the Community House, Town Hall and the [Juniper Library](/places/juniper-library/) around its edge; the library is open Wednesday to Sunday, closed Monday and Tuesday. Parades, craft fairs and concerts are held here, and in winter its trees carry holiday lights. Alcohol is not allowed in any Town park.

*Getting there:* on foot along Grand Avenue. *Best for:* a break for children, and the restrooms.

## 04 · Kauffman House Museum

The [Kauffman House Museum](/places/kauffman-house-museum/) at 407 Pitkin Street is an 1892 log hotel, the only one built in Grand Lake before 1900 that still stands, run by the Grand Lake Area Historical Society's volunteers. Its parlor and an upstairs bedroom are papered from a c. 1915 photograph, a section of wall still shows newspapers from 1893 and 1894, and the gallery's exhibits change. It is open Monday to Saturday 11 am–5 pm from Memorial Day through August, closed Sunday, then weekends in September and on special occasions. Adults $5, children 12 and under free.

*Getting there:* on foot from Town Square Park. *Best for:* half an hour on a summer day other than Sunday.

## 05 · Gene Stover Lakefront Park

[Gene Stover Lakefront Park](/places/gene-stover-lakefront-park/) at 1101 Lake Avenue is the Town's park and beach on the north shore of the lake, with two picnic pavilions, barbecue grills, public restrooms and the parking you started from. The pavilions are rented by the hour through the Town. From May to September, [Miyauchi's Snack Bar](/places/miyauchis-snack-bar/) on Lake Avenue makes small-batch ice cream on site and runs a grill; in June, July and August the ice cream counter is open daily 11 am–9 pm, and hours are shorter in May and September.

*Getting there:* on foot down to Lake Avenue. *Best for:* a picnic or an afternoon by the water.

## 06 · Cottage Court Museum and History Park

The [Cottage Court Museum](/places/cottage-court-museum-and-history-park/) at 725 Lake Avenue is a motor court built about 1915: four cabins with open carports between them under one roof, restored by the historical society as a museum of early automobile tourism. Visitors check in at the restored Eslick Store next door. The museum and store are open Monday to Saturday 11 am–5 pm from Memorial Day through August, closed Sunday, and Saturdays through September; adults $5, children 12 and under free, with a tour. The History Park beside it, with a pavilion, is free to use for a picnic.

*Getting there:* on foot along Lake Avenue. *Best for:* the second museum on the same summer day, and a picnic in the History Park.

## 07 · Dinner and a show

[Sagebrush BBQ & Grill](/places/sagebrush-bbq-and-grill/) at 1101 Grand Avenue has served barbecue, buffalo, steaks and burgers since 1999 and is open year-round, Sunday to Thursday 7 am–9 pm and Friday and Saturday to 10 pm, with a bar. [Rapids Lodge](/places/rapids-lodge/) at 210 Rapids Lane, on the Tonahutu River, serves dinner 5–9 pm (last seating 8:45) Tuesday to Sunday, closed Monday; dining-room reservations are online through Resy, and the bar and riverside seats are first come. [Rocky Mountain Repertory Theatre](/places/rocky-mountain-repertory-theatre/) at 800 Grand Avenue is the town's professional theatre, with a summer repertory season and fall and holiday shows; its box office opens Tuesday to Sunday in the fall, closed Monday, and is closed in winter, when the office across the street takes orders.

*Getting there:* Sagebrush and the theatre on foot along Grand Avenue; Rapids Lodge is on Rapids Lane by the river. *Best for:* the evening, any night but Monday if it is Rapids Lodge.

## A simple first visit

1. Park at Lakefront Park on Lake Avenue.
2. Breakfast at The Hub before the kitchen closes at 2:30.
3. The boardwalk shops, then Town Square Park.
4. In summer, the Kauffman House or the Cottage Court on any day but Sunday.
5. The beach, and ice cream at Miyauchi's from May to September.
6. Dinner at Sagebrush (any night) or Rapids Lodge (closed Monday), and a show if the theatre has one.

## Beyond the boardwalk

Three listings are a short drive away. [Adams Falls](/places/adams-falls-via-east-inlet-trail/) is in Rocky Mountain National Park, 0.3 mile from the East Inlet Trailhead at the end of West Portal Road, with 79 feet of climb; the Town's East Inlet page says there are restrooms at the trailhead. A park entrance pass is needed at any hour, and in 2026 a timed-entry reservation is required to enter between 9 am and 2 pm from May 22 through October 12, with the East Inlet Trail named among the areas it covers. The [Kawuneeche Visitor Center](/places/kawuneeche-visitor-center/) on US 34 north of town is open year-round, daily 9 am–5 pm, closed Thanksgiving and Christmas. [Point Park](/places/point-park/), on the channel to Shadow Mountain Reservoir, has a quarter-mile paved loop, covered picnic tables and a pier; it takes no reservations and has seven parking spaces.

## Getting here

Grand Lake is on US 34. [COtrip](https://www.cotrip.org/) carries the live state of the highways. Trail Ridge Road, US 34 through the national park, connects Grand Lake with Estes Park in summer; the park's operating-hours page says it is closed to through travel from mid-October to late May, and on 10 October 2026 its [Park Roads](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/road_status.htm) page said it was still open to through travel. The recorded Trail Ridge Road status line is 970-586-1222. Drive times vary with traffic, weather and construction; check a live routing service before you set out.

## Accessibility: what we can confirm

The Town lists public restrooms at Town Square Park and at Lakefront Park, and says Point Park is accessible, with 2 of its 7 parking spaces accessible. Rocky Mountain Repertory Theatre has wheelchair seating and assisted-listening devices; order by phone to arrange accessible seating.

Step-free entrances at the shops, cafés, restaurants and the two museums, and the state of the boardwalk and curb ramps on Grand Avenue, have not been confirmed and are not claimed here; contact a business directly before relying on it.
